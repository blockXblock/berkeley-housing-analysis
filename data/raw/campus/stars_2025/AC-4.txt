4.1 Applied learning for sustainability program
Does the institution make available solutions-focused applied learning or living laboratory experiences for students that address sustainability challenges?:
Yes


List or sample of currently active/available applied learning or living laboratory experiences for students that address sustainability challenges:

https://cleanenergycampus.berkeley.edu/living-lab

Living Lab Project Example:

The Berkeley Clean Energy Campus's Living Lab is a project that was initiated to drill a 400-foot-deep borehole to explore the feasibility of geothermal heating on campus. This project provided valuable learning and research opportunities, activating UC Berkeley's brain trust and fostering collaborative efforts among students, faculty, staff, and partners. The initiative aimed to find practical applications to real-world problems and develop solutions that can be tested, refined, and implemented in real-life settings. 

https://news.berkeley.edu/2022/03/30/uc-berkeley-drills-400-foot-borehole-to-explore-geothermal-heating-on-campus/



Are three or more institutional departments or units currently supporting solutions-focused applied learning or living laboratory experiences for students?:
Yes


Are there processes or tools in place to assess the success of the applied learning projects?:
Yes


Description of the processes or tools used to assess the success of the applied learning projects:
As part of the Berkeley Geothermal Project, students actively participated in: geological data collection, borehole drilling, and real-time logging; installation of distributed fiber optic sensing (DFOS) systems; and, conducting and interpreting Distributed Thermal Response Tests (DTRTs). Success of the project was determined based on the students’ ability to be involved in these hands-on tasks as part of the process of research. At the end of the project, students were expected to help complete key technical tasks, analyze real-time data, and contribute to the final system design recommendations. Students were also required to present findings in end-of-semester reports and group presentations, allowing structured reflection on sustainability outcomes and engineering challenges. At the same time, technical analyses provided additional assessment tools in terms of whether the project showed promise for supporting campus sustainability efforts. Those analyses included: DFOS data analysis to monitor subsurface temperature over time and depth. Analytical methods to estimate thermal conductivity and heat capacity of the ground. 3D hydrothermal modeling of the campus to simulate long-term system performance. 1D sensitivity analyses to validate simplified assumptions. Finally, and tied to the above analyses, the success of the living labs project was assessed based on the production of completed system feasibility studies, which projected that the geothermal system could meet nearly 100% of heating/cooling demand for a major campus node and reduce CO₂ emissions by over 10,000 tons annually.
