5.1 Sustainability literacy assessment design and administration
Has the institution conducted one or more assessments of the sustainability literacy of its students during the previous three years?:
Yes


Narrative overview of of the instruments/tools used to assess sustainability literacy:

A pre- and post- assessment was administered to a subset of the student body. The same cohort was administered the pre- and post-assessments via an online Google Form: Pre-assessment & Post-assessment.

 



Description of the institution’s recent sustainability literacy assessment findings and any notable trends:

The overall score improved by 5.6% over 3 months: from 70.6% to 76.2%. 

Average  improved from 10.6 to 11.4 points out of 15, and median improved from 11 to 12 points out of 15.

Frequently missed questions:

What is the most common cause of pollution of streams and rivers?

Changed from 37.8% to 40.7% correct answers.

Which of the following is a leading cause of the depletion of fish stocks in the Atlantic Ocean?

Changed from 40.5% to 64.8% correct answers.

The only question that did not show improvement pre vs post:

Which of the following is the best example of environmental justice?

Changed from 82% to 79.6%.

Were academic staff engaged in sustainability education at the institution involved in developing and/or adopting the methodologies used to assess sustainability literacy?:
Yes


Description of the process through which academic staff were involved in developing and/or adopting the methodologies used to assess sustainability literacy:

The sustainability literacy assessment was borrowed with permission from The Ohio State University Environmental and Social Sustainability Lab. More information about OSU's Assessment of Sustainability Knowledge (ASK) Scale can be found at the following link: https://ess.osu.edu/campus-sustainability-survey

One of the requirements is that the assessment should be administered to the same cohort of students in a pre- test and post- test. Three lecturers on campus administered the following sustainability assessment to their students in the beginning and end of their Fall semester course in 2023.

ESPM 98 (Pr. Spreyer) Environmental Justice Education

ESPM 198 (Pr. Arzbaecher) Solutions for a Sustainable and Just Future

GETH, NATRES 21, NATRES 24 (Pr. Rhew) Global Environmental Theme House

 

 

 



Are the literacy assessments designed and administered in such a way that the results can be used to evaluate the success of the institution’s sustainability education initiatives?:
Yes


Description of how the design and administration of the sustainability literacy assessments supports the evaluation of the success of the institution’s sustainability education initiatives:

Surveys are administered at the start and end of the academic semester. Students assessed were from a subset of those enrolleld in sustainability-focused course.

There were 12 questions in the survey designed to test student's literacy in sustaianble topics, and the students progress was documented after they completed a sustainabiltiy focused or related course.

5.2 Percentage of students assessed for sustainability literacy 
Percentage of students assessed for sustainability literacy, either directly or by representative sample:
75 to 100


Description of the process used to measure or estimate the percentage of students assessed for sustainability literacy:

Representative sample for this asessemnt was three courses that cover sustainability at different levels. A total of 111 students completed the pre-course sustainability literacy assessment in September-October 2023, and 54 students completed the post-course assessment in Novemeber- December 2023. While the total number of students enrolled in similar programs is not avaialable, the assessments provide a representative sample of students engaged in sustainability education.

Description of the Process

Survey Administration:

The literacy assessment was conducted via a Google Form distributed to students in a specific sustainability-focused course or program.
The questions were designed to assess foundational knowledge of environmental, social, and economic sustainability.

Survey Structure:

Adapted from The Ohio State University’s Assessment of Sustainability Knowledge (ASK) Scale, the survey included 12 multiple-choice questions covering key sustainability topics.

Sampling:

The pre-assessment (111 responses) was completed at the start of the course, while the post-assessment (54 responses) was conducted at its conclusion.
This approach targeted students actively engaged in sustainability-focused courses, providing insights into their knowledge levels and the course’s impact.
 A total of 139 students were registered for the three courses assessed, and all students were asked to participate in the assessment.The percentage of students assessed for sustainbiltiy literacy by representative sample is 80%.
