4.1 Sustainability culture assessment design and administration
Has the institution conducted an assessment of its sustainability culture during the previous three years?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL providing an overview of the instruments/tools used to assess sustainability culture:

In Spring 2023, UC Berkeley and UC Davis hosted a competition called the Cool Campus Challenge (CCC), which is a recurring competition with a new format. The CCC is held every few years, so the resulting surveys provide a longitudinal look at campus attitudes. 

One of the goals was to determine which UC Campus can do the most to reduce its carbon footprint. 

The Cool Campus Challenge (CCC) online dashboard enabled students to track and record their climate-friendly actions, including using sustainable transportation, reducing food waste, and conserving energy. The Spring 2023 competition ran from April 3 to April 28, with campuses competing for the title of "The Most Sustainable Campus" based on engagement levels.

In earlier versions, the CCC Survey asked participants the following:
- How long they plan to continue their pledged sustainable actions
- How worried they are about climate change
- The causes they believe are causing climate change
- Their belief that their individual actions can make a difference to help reduce climate change
- Their belief that our collective action on campus and in the UC System can make a difference to help reduce climate change

The goal of the CCC and its accompanying survey was to create and assess the culture of sustainability on each of UC campuses, helping the UC system reach carbon neutrality.

The campaign's core message was to help students understand their personal environmental impact. By engaging college students, it aimed to instill a foundational awareness that they could carry into their careers and adult lives, fostering a deeper connection to sustainability. The initiative focused on three key utilities—energy, water, and waste—while using a pledge and points system to encourage direct action.

 



Is the culture assessment designed and administered in such a way that the results can be used to measure change over time?:
Yes


Description of how the design and administration of the sustainability culture assessments supports the measurement of change over time:

The 2023 CCC competition involved a significantly lower participation from students and staff compared to previous years. One of the reasons is its grassroots nature and lack of system-wide marketing. Only two UC Campuses particiapted in the CCC in 2023.

Back in April 2019, all 10 campuses across the University of California system participated in the Cool Campus Challenge to see which UC location can do the most to reduce its carbon footprint. Participants earned 500 points for completing an online research and evaluation survey. This action was among the top ten most popular actions taken during the Cool Campus Challenge. In 2019, a total of 5,106 participants across the UC system completed the survey, including faculty, staff, and students. Comparatively, in 2015, a total of 3,667 participants across the UC system completed the survey for an average response rate of 19% (22% by staff and 14% by students).

Interestingly, the majority response to the statement "My individual actions can make a difference to help reduce climate change" switched from "Strongly agree" in the 2015 CCC to "Agree" in the 2019 CCC.

4.2 Percentage of students assessed for sustainability culture
Percentage of students assessed for sustainability culture, either directly or by representative sample:
1 to 24


Description of the process used to measure or estimate the percentage of students assessed for sustainability culture:

Student participation was measured by their participation in the CCC Dashboards and their engagement level. 

The lead at UC Berkeley secured support from two other students, one of which worked for HADSA (Housing and Dining Services). This was a beneficial connection that linked the CCC to at least one campus administration and led to successful use of tabling resources. Due to its limited resources and grassroots nature, the Cool Campus Challenge at UC Berkeley did not target all students directly but instead relied on outreach of the Housing and Dining Services Administration (HADSA). This limited the pool of potential participants to those reached through this channel, which is estimated to be 200, based on the tabling, andTGIF grant to produce traction via flyers.

Only 40 (20%) individuals registered for CCC at UC Berkeley and of those, 9 individuals earned 700+ points, representing active engagement beyond initial signup. Thus the engagement rate for UC Berkeley participants in the Cool Campus Challenge was 22.5%.
