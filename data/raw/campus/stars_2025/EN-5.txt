5.2 Employee community service program
Does the institution have one or more programs designed to support employee community service?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL providing an overview of the institution’s programs to support employee community service:

UC Berkeley has programs designed to support employee community service. One key initiative is the Cal Civic Engagement Program, which encourages and supports faculty and staff in contributing to the community through volunteerism and civic engagement. This program aligns with the university’s mission to promote social responsibility and community involvement. Cal Employees: Community at Work: This program encourages UC Berkeley employees to participate in community service through university-organized events and opportunities, such as days of service, local non-profit partnerships, and volunteering initiatives.

Day of Service Events: UC Berkeley frequently organizes "days of service," where employees can volunteer their time to work with local organizations on community service projects, such as environmental clean-ups, mentoring youth, or supporting non-profits. Paid Time Off for Volunteering: Certain departments and units at UC Berkeley offer flexible work arrangements or paid time off for employees to engage in community service activities, supporting a work-life balance that encourages giving back.

 



Does the institution support employees volunteering for community organizations during regular work hours?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL providing an overview of the institution’s support for community volunteering during regular work hours:

Staff are offered release time for campus-sponsored activities, including activities in Berkeley Staff Organizations. Release Time for Campus-Sponsored Activities: In the normal course of business, exempt employees should receive at least 10 workdays of approved paid release time annually, and non-exempt employees should receive at least 80 hours of approved paid release time annually to participate in campus-sponsored activities. Release time cannot be accrued for carry-forward to the next year.

https://campuspol.berkeley.edu/policies/stafforgs.pdf

The University Section Club, a non-profit UC Berkeley support group, is one example of a Berkeley Staff Organization. Its mission and purpose includes:
- To provide service to the University Community
- To raise funds to aid deserving students, support student-related groups, and foster scholarship and international understanding

Volunteer activities welcome and support students, international scholars and their families. Fundraising activities, dues and donations support philanthropy to students which include emergency grants, financial support for student groups and programs, and two endowed scholarships.

https://stafforg.berkeley.edu/university-section-club-contact-and-officers

Berkeley Veterans mission is to serve veterans at UC Berkeley and the surrounding communities, and to serve the campus by acting as a bridge to the military and veterans community.

Goals: Participating in/sponsoring campus service projects, such as cleanup efforts

https://stafforg.berkeley.edu/organizations/identity-based-staff-orgs/berkeley-veterans

LavenderCal is UC Berkeley’s network for lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and queer+ employees. We are staff, faculty, librarians, academic researchers, postdoctoral appointees, and visiting scholars. We welcome the support and participation of allies. LavenderCal is recognized as an official campus staff organization, supported by the Chancellor and the Vice Chancellor for Equity & Inclusion.

Goal: To sustain a large, inclusive network of staff and faculty who volunteer effort—little or much, occasionally or regularly—to promote the welfare of the UC LGBTQ+ communities

https://stafforg.berkeley.edu/staff-organizations/identity-based-staff-orgs/lavendercal

UC Berkeley Wellness Ambassadors are staff or faculty volunteers who play a vital role in supporting Be Well at Work – Wellness Program by promoting health and wellness programs to your coworkers, department, or friends on campus. Any UC Berkeley faculty or staff can volunteer to be a Wellness Ambassador. Wellness Ambassadors volunteer during regular work-time hours.

https://uhs.berkeley.edu/facstaff/wellness/wellness-ambassadors

Staff are invited to volunteer at Summerfest, a highlight of Staff Appreciation Week. The event is an opportunity for friends, co-workers, and colleagues to enjoy a delicious free lunch, score a free commemorative t-shirt, learn about staff organizations, and come together to celebrate their vital contributions to the university. It’s also a way for the university to sincerely thank the staff for their service. With permission from their supervisor, volunteers contribute their time during regular work hours.

https://hr.berkeley.edu/compensation-benefits/perks/staff-appreciation-week

The Dean of Students portfolio in Student Affairs offers an Externship program for staff, which supports staff members from a department to volunteer as an Extern for another department. This externship volunteer program takes place during the regular work hours, so volunteers are compensated for their time.

UC Berkeley also provides employee incentives and positive recognition such as the Chancellor's Awards for Public Service and a staff public service award. Individual departments also offer their own community service awards.

5.3 Support for public policies to advance sustainability
Have one or more individuals affiliated with the institution helped develop public policies that address sustainability challenges within the previous three years?:
Yes


Narrative outlining how individuals affiliated with the institution have helped develop local or regional public policies that address sustainability challenges:

As a public university, UC Berkeley and its leaders are not permitted to endorse specific legislation.

However, UC Berkeley provides a place for individual scholars, and teams of scholars, to create and share knowledge, and make policy recommendations. When in government service, the campus often provides compensation to faculty members in a position to use their scholarship to inform rational policy making.

The Chancellor made a strong statement of support for sustainability in this endorsement of CalPIRG efforts, and has made it the policy of UC Berkeley to pursue a carbon-free energy system, devoting over half a billion dollars to a new clean energy project. In phase 1, the project aims to reduce campus greenhouse gas emissions by over 70%.

Our Vice Chancellor for Administration spoke about sustainability commitments in this speech.

Additionally, the campus supported the City of Berkeley Southside Complete Streets Project, which aims to make the city streets adjacent to the southside of campus more pedestrian and bike friendly, and includes dedicated bus lanes. 

Faculty and students advocated for the following bills, which were approved: 

Senate Bill 1053

Senate Bill 707

Assembly Bill 3264

Assembly Bill 1866

Assembly Bill 660

https://haas.berkeley.edu/sustainability/campus-sustainability/zero-waste-2/

Assembly Bill 1042

 



Narrative outlining how individuals affiliated with the institution have helped develop national or international public policies that address sustainability challenges:

Researchers affiliated with UC Berkeley have been awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for their work in informing policy in climate change through the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change. Within the past three years, several faculty members served in White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (Director of the White House’s Greenhouse Gas Monitoring Program) and on the President’s Council of Science Advisors, advising the president on policy sustainability and science. Another faculty member served as United States Department of the Treasury’s Deputy Assistant Secretary for Climate and Energy Economics to make policy recommendations for the US.

UC Berkeley has had a substantial impact on public policy in sustainability, both nationally and internationally, through faculty research, student initiatives, and collaborative projects. Here are some examples:

Climate Policy and Carbon Emission Standards: Researchers at UC Berkeley, especially from the Goldman School of Public Policy and the Energy and Resources Group, have developed frameworks that informed carbon emission policies. Their work has influenced key standards in California, such as the cap-and-trade program and emission reduction goals, which have been used as models for other states and countries.

Energy Efficiency and Renewable Energy: Faculty and students have been involved in creating the scientific groundwork for energy efficiency standards, particularly for appliances and buildings. For example, Berkeley's contributions helped shape the federal Energy Star program, and the work done on renewable energy transitions has informed policy proposals in the EU and beyond.

Environmental Justice: The Berkeley Environmental Law Clinic and faculty from the School of Law have supported environmental justice through legal frameworks and policy recommendations. Their research has led to legislation that addresses pollution and environmental health disparities in vulnerable communities.

Sustainable Agriculture and Land Use: Berkeley's College of Natural Resources has pioneered research on sustainable agriculture and land use, with an emphasis on reducing agricultural emissions, water conservation, and soil health. Faculty members have advised the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) on sustainable farming practices, which in turn influence international agricultural sustainability guidelines.

Waste Management and Circular Economy: Faculty and students at UC Berkeley have conducted research on waste management strategies, advocating for a shift toward a circular economy. These insights have contributed to California's waste reduction policies and served as models for policies internationally, particularly in regions looking to reduce landfill waste and improve recycling standards.

Transportation and Sustainable Urban Planning: The Institute of Transportation Studies at Berkeley has developed models and guidelines for sustainable urban planning, particularly in transit-oriented development and emissions reduction in transportation. Their research has informed policy changes in California and provided insights for international urban planning organizations.
