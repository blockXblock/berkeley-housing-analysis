6.1 Sustainability-focused community partnerships
Does the institution have at least one community partnership that is sustainability-focused?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL providing an overview of the institution’s sustainability-focused community partnerships:

Chancellor's Community Partnerships fund: 

The UC Berkeley Chancellor’s Community Partnership Fund (the Fund) invests in projects and programs that establish new or existing partnerships between UC Berkeley and Berkeley community entities that improve the quality of life for people who live or work in Berkeley. The Fund seeks partnerships and collaborations that are designed to match the university’s expertise and service with community wisdom and leadership. Eligibility is limited to projects and programs that have at least one community partner and one UC Berkeley partner.  An ideal campus/community partnership gives students, faculty and staff an opportunity to bring the university’s public service mission to life in community projects that further social justice and racial equity. Projects that benefit specific neighborhoods or groups of Berkeleyans are preferred.

College Corps: 

#CaliforniansForAll College Corps is a statewide partnership with California Volunteers to create debt-free pathways to college while mobilizing thousands of college students to tackle pressing community issues. At UC Berkeley, approximately 100 undergraduates are serving with community partners supporting food and climate justice, K-12 education, and behavioral health & wellness efforts.

6.2 Partnerships to support underrepresented groups and vulnerable populations
Does the institution have at least one community partnership that explicitly aims to support underrepresented groups and/or vulnerable populations in addressing the sustainability challenges they have identified?:
Yes


Description of the institution’s community partnerships to support underrepresented groups and/or vulnerable populations in addressing sustainability challenges:

In 2023-24, through College Corps, a state partnership funded by California Volunteers, the UC Public Service Center placed 46 undergraduate student Fellows in Climate or Food Justice community partnerships with 10 different community organizations. Each Fellow served for 450 hours over the course of the year, earning $10,000 in living stipends and education awards. Partner organizations included: The Center (Oakland Unified School District's demonstration garden and test kitchen), GRID Alternatives, Frontline Catalysts, Dorothy Day House, Berkeley Meals on Wheels, the Berkeley Student Food Collective, and Climate Emergency Mobilization Task Force, among others.

Description of the institution’s approach to community partnership assessment and how the results are used to improve reciprocity and mutual benefit:

UC Berkeley’s Chancellor’s Community Partnership Fund (CCPF) employs a structured approach to assessing and improving community partnerships, ensuring reciprocity and mutual benefit through the following key practices:

Assessment through Thematic Objectives:
Partnerships are evaluated based on their alignment with six thematic areas, such as education, health, and environmental stewardship. Applications are assessed for their ability to address systemic barriers, advance equity, and improve the quality of life for Berkeley residents.

Collaborative Structure:
All projects must involve significant collaboration between UC Berkeley and at least one eligible community partner. Assessments emphasize the depth, sustainability, and potential for long-term impact of these partnerships.

Focus on Equity-Driven Policies:
The assessment process prioritizes projects that align with equity-driven initiatives developed through extensive community engagement, such as the Berkeley Youth Equity Partnership. This ensures that projects directly address the needs of underserved populations.

Use of Assessment Results for Improvement:

Assessment results inform strategies to enhance the effectiveness and sustainability of community-campus collaborations.
Feedback from the CCPF Advisory Board guides partners in refining their objectives and implementation plans, strengthening future applications.
The fund’s emphasis on leveraging additional resources fosters the development of partnerships that extend beyond reliance on CCPF support.

This approach ensures that partnerships between UC Berkeley and the Berkeley community are equitable, impactful, and aligned with shared goals, fostering mutual growth and continuous improvement.

It is governed by the Strategic Academic Plan that UC Berkeley adopted in 2018.
