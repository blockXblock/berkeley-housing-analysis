9.1 Inter-campus collaboration for sustainability
Does the institution participate in one or more sustainability-focused networks for educational institutions?:
Yes


Description of the institution’s participation in sustainability-focused networks for educational institutions:

Association for the Advancement of Sustainability in Higher Education (AASHE): UC Berkeley is a member of AASHE and participates in its Sustainability Tracking, Assessment & Rating System (STARS). AASHE provides resources, workshops, and a forum for institutions to collaborate on sustainability efforts. Berkeley’s STARS assessment has allowed the university to benchmark its performance in areas such as energy, waste, and transportation against other institutions. 

Sustainable Purchasing Leadership Council (SPLC): UC Berkeley has integrated SPLC’s principles into its procurement processes to promote sustainable purchasing. The SPLC provides guidance on socially and environmentally responsible purchasing, and UC Berkeley uses these standards to select products and services that minimize environmental impact, support fair labor practices, and reduce waste. This is particularly important in areas such as laboratory supplies, electronics, and office materials, where sustainable purchasing can significantly reduce the university’s overall environmental footprint. 

Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design (LEED): UC Berkeley has made extensive use of LEED standards to guide sustainable building practices on campus. Many buildings at UC Berkeley, including new construction and major renovations, have been designed to meet LEED certification standards, which focus on energy efficiency, water conservation, indoor environmental quality, and sustainable site development. UC Berkeley's LEED-certified buildings contribute to reduced greenhouse gas emissions, lower operating costs, and improved indoor environments for students and staff. The campus aims to meet at least LEED Silver standards for new constructions, and several buildings have achieved LEED Gold or higher.

National Association of Educational Procurement (NAEP) is another organization UC Berkeley is involved with, particularly regarding sustainable procurement practices. NAEP is a professional association that provides guidance and resources for procurement professionals in higher education. Through NAEP, UC Berkeley can access tools and best practices to promote sustainable purchasing across campus operations, aligning with its sustainability goals.

DOE Better Climate Challenge is another significant program that aligns with UC Berkeley's sustainability and climate goals, and it would indeed count as part of its engagement in broader sustainability-focused initiatives. By participating in the Better Climate Challenge, UC Berkeley commits to reducing its greenhouse gas emissions and improving energy efficiency, contributing to the Department of Energy's goal of helping organizations achieve at least a 50% reduction in emissions over 10 years. Through this program, UC Berkeley benefits from resources, technical support, and a network of peers also aiming to meet ambitious climate targets. Participation demonstrates UC Berkeley’s dedication to transparency in tracking emissions reductions and serves as a model for other universities and organizations pursuing aggressive climate action.



Has the institution formally shared its sustainability experiences and lessons learned with other institutions during the previous three years?:
Yes


Narrative outlining how the institution formally shares its sustainability experiences and lessons learned with other institutions:

UC Berkeley actively shares its sustainability experiences and insights with other institutions.

One of the ways is through AASHE (Association for the Advancement of Sustainability in Higher Education) conferences. Over the past three years, many sustainability leaders from UC Berkeley have presented at these conferences, discussing various topics like carbon neutrality, green labs, sustainable procurement, and climate resilience. These presentations have served as platforms for UC Berkeley to highlight both its achievements and the challenges it has encountered, providing valuable lessons for other AASHE members.

UC Berkeley Procurement Officer, Russell Chung, has given presentations at Economist Sustainability Week in NYC, Insead University SF Campus, and at SPLC 2022 sustainability Conference in Atlanta. 

UC Berkeley particiaptes in the UN’s Principles for Responsible Management Education (PRME) initiative that brings together the UN and business schools around the world to “inspire and champion responsible management education, research, and thought leadership globally” through the application of six principles: purpose, values, method, research, partnership, and dialogue.

https://haas.berkeley.edu/sustainability/thought-leadership/prme/

UC Berkeley Energy Office presented on UCB experience with transitioning to an electrified heating and cooling plant at three conferences in 2024.
