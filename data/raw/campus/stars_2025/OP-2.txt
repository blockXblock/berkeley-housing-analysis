Description of the institution’s certified green cleaning service:

https://facilities.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/fs_ucberkeley_green_cleaning_program_2010.pdf

UC Berkeley’s certified green cleaning service is structured around industry-leading environmental standards to ensure a sustainable, health-conscious approach to campus cleanliness. According to UC Berkeley’s Green Cleaning Program document, the program follows these specific standards:

Green Seal GS-42: This standard outlines requirements for cleaning service providers to use environmentally responsible products and methods. UC Berkeley’s cleaning program meets GS-42 criteria by using certified low-toxicity cleaning products and implementing pollution-prevention practices.

LEED-EBOM (Existing Buildings: Operations & Maintenance): Many campus buildings meet LEED-EBOM standards, which emphasize green cleaning practices that contribute to overall building sustainability, including the use of sustainable materials, improved air quality, and waste reduction strategies.

EPA Safer Choice: The program includes products certified by the EPA’s Safer Choice program, verifying that these products are safer for human health and the environment.

Narrative outlining how building-level energy performance is assessed and how the results are used to improve performance:

Energy performance is tracked and assessed at the building level on at least an annual basis to locate inefficiency and confirm persistence of performance from recent improvements. Tools to support these assessments include benchmarking platform (ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager), energy management systems, and building performance dashboards.



Floor area of existing buildings individually assessed for indoor environmental quality (IEQ) performance:
1,503,129.29 Square meters


Narrative outlining how building-level IEQ is assessed and how the results are used to improve performance:

UC Berkeley is very aware of the importance of Indoor Environmental Quality and has many programs in place to support it.

Facilities Services maintains and services HVAC equipment.  The preventive maintenance program includes the routine replacement of filters, belts and lubrication.

Where appropriate, the highest level of MERV filtration has been installed.

All cooling towers are inspected monthly with support from a chemical maintenance and testing company.  Cooling towers are also cleaned annually.

Thermal conditions are monitored through centrally controlled building management systems by a dedicated controls team in Facilities Services.

EH&S responds to and investigates air quality concerns such as mold, odor, particulates and chemical concerns.   They have instrumentation to measure a wide range of sources and conditions.   If needed, EH&S and Facilities Services work together to address the condition and control the hazard.

UC Berkeley design standards include requirements for low VOC materials.   Construction projects on campus are required to perform activities to limit air quality impact through such measures as limiting idling and controlling dust.

Campus custodial and grounds operations use green products and limit chemical use. 

Facilities Services has an asbestos abatement program which works closely with trade teams and capital projects to prevent exposure.  

UC Berkeley has a robust Integrated Pest Management program.

Facilities Services and EH&S collaborate on a refrigeration monitoring program that complies with Environmental Protection Agency, California Air Resources Board, and Bay Area Air Quality Board requirements.

Buildings that are individually assessed for indoor environmental quality (IEQ) performance include those that are LEED-certified. LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design) certification includes requirements for assessing and improving indoor environmental quality, including factors such as air quality, lighting, acoustics, and materials used within the building.

Narrative outlining how building-level water performance is assessed and how the results are used to improve performance:

UC Berkeley assesses building-level water performance through guidelines and standards designed to optimize water efficiency. The UC Berkeley Sustainability Building Guidelines (2024) and Campus Design Standards (2020) provide frameworks for incorporating water-efficient systems in construction and renovation projects, such as low-flow fixtures and smart irrigation systems.

The data collected through these tools is used to:

Track and Benchmark Performance: Water audits and real-time monitoring help identify inefficiencies and set performance benchmarks.
Inform Policies and Processes: Results drive updates to water-use policies and guidelines for retrofitting buildings to meet higher conservation standards.
Guide Infrastructure Upgrades: Data supports targeted investments in water-saving technologies, like efficient fixtures and water recycling systems.
Ensure Ongoing Improvement: The data is integrated into sustainability reporting and guides continuous refinement of water conservation efforts.
