Description of the methodology or calculator used to conduct the scope 1 and 2 GHG emissions inventory:

UC Berkeley reports on ten emissions sources and analyzes emissions in three different categories:

Scope 1 - Direct Emissions: natural gas from the campus cogeneration (heating and cooling) plant, purchased natural gas, emergency generators, campus fleet, emissions from refrigerants
Scope 2 - Indirect Emissions: purchased electricity
Scope 3 - Indirect Emissions: business air travel, student commute, faculty/staff commute, solid waste, water

The campus reports its GHG inventory to both The Climate Registry and the California Air Resources Board (CARB). 

The UC Berkeley inventory includes all six major greenhouse gases. Scope 1 and 2 adhere to The Climate Registry protocol. Scope 3 emissions use Clean Air Cool Planet - Campus Carbon Calculator methodology/factors.

The geographic boundary for the inventory is generally defined as those buildings central to the University mission and under operational control of the campus. This includes central campus buildings, student housing and off-central campus facilities owned by the University.

The campus has performed LCA of emissions from procurement and construction on various occasions: http://sustainability.berkeley.edu/calcap/calcap-ghg-inventory

Carbon storage from on-site composting:
0.05 Metric tons of CO2 equivalent


Description of the institution’s carbon storage from on-site composting:

As per Residential & Student Services Program in Berkeley Housing and Diniging, Chef Peter estimated about 400 pounds per year is composted internally. 400 poounds of food waste is 0.1814metric tons. 

We may assume a standard emission factor of 0.25 MTCO₂e per metric ton of food waste composted (methane emissions avoided by diverting from landfill).

Avoided Methane Emissions (MTCO₂e) are 0.0454MTCO₂e.

Additionally, we can calculate a carbon sequestration factor. If compost is applied to gardens, add a carbon sequestration factor of ~0.05 MTCO₂e per metric ton of compost applied, which makes it 0.0091MTCO₂e

So, in total, composting 400 pounds of food waste annually avoids approximately 0.0545 MTCO₂e (or 54.5 kg CO₂e) each year. This calculation accounts for methane avoidance and optional carbon sequestration.
