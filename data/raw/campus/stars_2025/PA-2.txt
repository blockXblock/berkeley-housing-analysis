2.1 Public sustainability commitment
Has the institution made a public commitment to sustainability, as evidenced by an external commitment or a published plan?:
Yes


Narrative detailing the institution’s guiding vision or goals for sustainability and the plan(s) in which they are published:

UC Berkeley Climate Action Plan: UC Berkeley has a published Climate Action Plan that details its strategies to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and meet climate goals. This plan outlines steps for achieving energy efficiency, promoting renewable energy, and supporting sustainable transportation options on campus.

Second Nature Climate Leadership Commitments: UC Berkeley is a signatory to the Climate Leadership Commitments by Second Nature, which includes the Carbon Commitment. This external pledge reinforces the university's commitment to reducing carbon emissions and building resilience in the face of climate change.

UC Berkeley Sustainability Plan: The university has published a Sustainability Plan that includes specific targets and goals for areas such as energy, water, waste reduction, sustainable food systems, and biodiversity. The plan is aligned with the broader UC system’s sustainability policies but includes campus-specific goals and timelines.

 IRAP, Campus Master Plan, LRDP



Narrative and/or website URL outlining the institution’s external sustainability commitments that include a reporting requirement:

STARS Reporting through AASHE: UC Berkeley is a participant in the Sustainability Tracking, Assessment & Rating System (STARS), which is managed by AASHE. By completing and publishing STARS assessments, UC Berkeley transparently shares its sustainability performance and progress. The campus's ongoing participation in STARS reflects its commitment to continual improvement and accountability in sustainability.

The Climate Registry (TCR): UC Berkeley reports greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions data to The Climate Registry, a non-profit organization that provides standardized methods for measuring and reporting carbon emissions. Through TCR, UC Berkeley submits verified annual GHG inventories, which are then made publicly accessible. This reporting helps ensure transparency and accuracy in tracking emissions over time.

UC Berkeley’s 2018 Strategic Academic Plan outlines signature initiatives that analyze the critical issues and challenges facing our state, our nation, and our world that UC Berkeley is particularly well suited to address. One of the six signature initiatives is Environmental Change, Sustainability, and Justice.

The campus is activating this signature environmental initiative through its latest capital campaign, Light the Way. Leads for this initiative also share with potential contributors information on planning efforts for developing a zero-carbon campus energy system.

Has the institution adopted one or more measurable sustainability objectives that address campus operations?:
Yes


Narrative listing the institution’s measurable sustainability objectives that address campus operations:

UC Berkeley follows the UC systemwide Sustainable Practices Policy. Section III. C. outlines the goals that all UC locations have to meet, including reducing all emissions by at least 90% by 2045 and eliminating the remaining emissions through carbon capture and storage credits (section III.C.1.).

UC Berkeley's Sustainability Plan includes a section on Climate & Resiliency which outlines plans to "accelerate a just and equitable transition to a resilient, clean energy, and zero carbon campus." UC Berkeley has already set ambitious greenhouse gas emissions reduction targets and identified multiple strategies for achieving the target.

Waste goals for UC:
- Achieve zero waste by prioritizing reduce, reuse, and then recycle and compost (or other forms of organic recycling) by the following: Reduce 25% per capita from FY 2015/16 levels by 2025
- Reduce 50% per capita from FY 2015/16 levels by 2030
- Divert 90% of municipal solid waste from the landfill

UC Berkeley-specific waste goals:
- Eliminate all non-essential single use plastic for which there is a viable alternative by end of calendar year 2030

UC goals for food & dining:
- By 2030, 25% of food spend will be on sustainable food* products while maintaining accessibility and affordability for all students

Moreover, the Berkeley Clean Energy Campus initiative states the following strategies: 
- Reduce 90% GHG Emissions from 2019 by 2045.  
- Support the planning and efforts to update the main campus heating, cooling and
power system to operate primarily on clean electricity by 2030. This major capital
improvement can reduce campus emissions by 60-80%.
- Reduce short-lived climate pollutants including black carbon and methane.
- Support the phase out hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) by 2030.
- Engage in efforts to increase telework options for employees and improve campus
space utilization.

https://sustainability.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/uc_berkeley_sustainability_plan_2020_1.pdf

 
The 2020 Green Labs Action Plan identifies near-term initiatives and responsibilities as well as longer-range ideas for Green Labs. The Green Labs Program was created to improve sustainable practices within research and teaching labs on campus with the goal to incorporate the use of water reduction, waste elimination and energy saving technology, along with actions and procurement strategies for researchers, instructors, and lab spaces.

Every year Green Labs will focus on manageable projects within key areas identified by the program to aid the larger theme of sustainability that accomplishes water, energy or waste reduction in campus labs. Key actions & targets for 2020-2022 are outlined in the Green Labs Action Plan.

https://sustainability.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/uc_berkeley_green_labs_action_plan_may_2020.pdf


The 2019 Zero Waste Plan outlines a variety of different strategies and approaches to achieve zero waste. 

Cal Zero Waste has expanded and grown the current portfolio of programs. Zero Waste October includes interactive and engagement elements; Cal Zero Waste will facilitate more conversations with Capital Projects to increase collaboration. Cal Zero Waste also facilitates conversations between procurement and also Green Labs to work on zero waste in laboratories and research spaces on campus.

https://facilities.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/2019_uc_berkeley_zero_waste_plan_final.pdf

 
The 2021 Long Range Development Plan (LRDP) describes a framework for land use and investment to meet the academic goals and objectives of the University. The companion Environmental Impact Report provides information on the environmental implications of the LRDP and includes an extended treatment of potential impacts and mitigation best practices. 

Goal: Plan every new project to serve as a model of resource conservation and environmental stewardship (LRDP)

Key Strategies:
- Implement an ongoing program of investment to restore and renew the campus park landscape; implement a program of strategic investment in new and enhanced campus open park spaces. (LRDP)
- Continue to manage runoff into storm drain systems such that the aggregate effect of projects implementing the 2020 LRDP is no net increase in runoff over existing conditions. (LRDP EIR)
- Continue to revise and implement the Strawberry Creek Management Plan (SCMP) to include recommendations for habitat restoration and enhancement along specific segments of the creek. (LRDP EIR)
- Continue implementing an urban runoff management program as published in the Strawberry Creek Management Plan. (LRDP EIR)
- Manage the natural preserves based on ecological principles, including replacing invasive exotic plants with native plants suited to this biotic zone, replacing unhealthy plants and plants at the ends of their natural lives, and preserving and enhancing the habitat value of the zone. (LRDP)

Has the institution adopted one or more measurable sustainability objectives that address racial equity and/or social justice?:
Yes


Narrative listing the institution’s measurable sustainability objectives that address racial equity and/or social justice:

Supplier Diversity Goals: UC Berkeley has measurable objectives to enhance supplier diversity as part of its sustainable procurement strategy. The university actively works to increase purchasing from businesses owned by underrepresented groups, including minority-owned, women-owned, and veteran-owned businesses. These goals align with both sustainability and social justice by creating economic opportunities for historically marginalized communities.

- 25% Economically and Socially Responsible Spend as a total percentage of addressable spend target to be reached within five (5) fiscal years of adoption of this section in the UC Sustainable Procurement Guidelines

Diversity, Equity, Inclusion, and Belonging (DEIB) Initiatives: UC Berkeley’s Office of Diversity, Equity, Inclusion, and Belonging has established measurable objectives to promote racial equity, including increasing the representation of underrepresented groups among faculty, staff, and students. These goals often intersect with sustainability, as the university seeks to ensure that sustainability efforts are inclusive and beneficial to all communities.

Equity and Inclusion in Climate Resilience Planning: UC Berkeley incorporates equity and social vulnerability considerations into its climate resilience planning. This includes identifying and addressing how climate impacts may disproportionately affect marginalized communities on campus and in surrounding areas. Measurable outcomes include establishing support systems, resources, and protections for vulnerable populations affected by climate-related disruptions. the UC Office of the President has developed a "Framework for J.E.D.I.-Centered Climate Resilience Planning," highlighting the importance of justice, equity, diversity, and inclusion in addressing climate vulnerabilities.

Housing and Food Security Initiatives: UC Berkeley has set objectives to address food and housing insecurity among its student population, recognizing these as issues of social and racial equity. Programs like the Basic Needs Center aim to reduce food insecurity and homelessness among students, many of whom are from underrepresented backgrounds. The success of these programs is measured through the number of students served and reductions in food and housing insecurity statistics.

 

 



Has the institution adopted one or more measurable sustainability objectives that address administration and/or finance?:
Yes


Narrative listing the institution’s measurable sustainability objectives that address administration and/or finance:

University of California sustainable purchasing requirements, as found in the UC Sustainable Practices Policy, are included below. UC Berkeley's Sustainability Plan requires the campus follow these larger UC-wide purchasing guidelines.

- 100% compliance with Required Level Green Spend criteria within three (3) fiscal years of the addition of those products and/or product categories to the Guidelines.
- 25% Green Spend as a total percentage of spend per product category; target to be reached within three (3) fiscal years after a category is added to the Guidelines.

Each University’s Procurement department will integrate sustainability into its processes and practices, including competitive solicitations, in order to satisfy the sustainable purchasing goals outlined above for products, as well as for the procurement of services.

The University will do so by:
a. Allocating a minimum of 15% of the points utilized in solicitation evaluations to sustainability criteria. Criteria may include, but is not limited to, sustainable product attributes, supplier diversity, supplier practices, contributions to health and wellbeing, and materials safety.

https://policy.ucop.edu/doc/3100155/SustainablePractices

Fair Wage/Fair Work Program: As part of the UC Fair Wage/Fair Work Plan, UC Berkeley ensures that 100% of workers employed by the university or its contractors receive a minimum wage above the state-mandated rate. This initiative reflects UC Berkeley’s commitment to social justice by improving economic equity and setting measurable standards for fair compensation.

Sustainable Procurement Policy: UC Berkeley has adopted a Sustainable Procurement Policy that sets measurable goals for purchasing environmentally preferable products and services. Objectives include increasing the percentage of certified green products, reducing single-use plastics, and prioritizing suppliers with sustainable practices. Progress is measured by tracking procurement data, allowing the university to report on its sustainable spending and supplier diversity goals.

Investment Strategies for Climate Responsibility: The University of California system, which includes UC Berkeley, has committed to carbon neutrality in its investment portfolio and has divested from fossil fuels. UC Berkeley’s financial policies include objectives to support environmentally responsible investments, aligning its endowment and financial holdings with climate and sustainability goals. Progress is measured through annual investment disclosures, tracking reductions in carbon-intensive assets, and increasing investments in sustainable and renewable sectors.

Green Building Standards: UC Berkeley requires all new construction and major renovations to meet 100% LEED Silver standards or higher. This objective ties administrative decision-making in facilities management and capital projects directly to sustainability goals. Compliance is tracked through certifications, with projects measured on criteria like energy efficiency, water conservation, and materials sourcing.

Energy Efficiency and Cost Savings: UC Berkeley’s energy management objectives aim to reduce energy consumption and costs across campus operations. Programs under these objectives include energy retrofitting, installing renewable energy sources, and participating in demand response programs to reduce peak energy usage. These efforts are monitored through measurable indicators such as kilowatt-hour reductions, greenhouse gas emissions savings, and financial savings reported in the campus's annual sustainability report.

Sustainable Budgeting and Resource Allocation: UC Berkeley has taken steps to integrate sustainability into its budgeting processes. For example, the university allocates funds specifically for sustainability initiatives, such as energy efficiency upgrades and green lab certifications, and measures the impact of these investments. Funding for sustainability is monitored and assessed based on program outcomes, such as emissions reductions and operational savings.
