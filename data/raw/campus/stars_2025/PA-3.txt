3.1 Student participation in governance
Does the institution have one or more ongoing bodies through which students can democratically participate in the governance of the institution?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL outlining the ongoing bodies through which students can democratically participate in the governance of the institution:

Associated Students of the University of California, Chancellor's Office, Student Environmental Resource Center, Zero Waste Coalition, Graduate Assembly, Office of Student Affairs, and the Housing and Dining division all appoint one student seat each on CACS and each have one vote. 

https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1Es6tMkaGrcUlrpL5yGm9J8UZVpfjOOvR/edit?usp=sharing&ouid=105826157914045237014&rtpof=true&sd=true

UC Berkeley has several ongoing bodies that allow students to democratically participate in institutional governance. These bodies enable students to have a voice in decision-making processes, especially around policies and initiatives that impact campus life, sustainability, and other areas of interest to the student community. Key examples include:

Associated Students of the University of California (ASUC) - The ASUC is UC Berkeley’s official student government and one of the most autonomous student governments in the country. It includes an executive branch, senate, and judicial council, all of which are elected by the student body. ASUC representatives advocate for student interests and work on policy issues that affect the campus community.

Graduate Assembly (GA) - Similar to the ASUC, the GA serves as the representative body for graduate students at Berkeley. It focuses on issues specific to graduate students and provides a platform for their concerns in university governance.

Student Advisory Committees - UC Berkeley has various advisory committees that include student representatives. These may focus on topics such as sustainability, mental health, housing, and campus safety. Examples include the Chancellor's Advisory Committee on Sustainability and the Student Advisory Committee on Mental Health.

UC Berkeley Academic Senate Committees - Some Academic Senate committees, which play a role in academic and administrative governance, include student representatives. These committees can influence decisions on curriculum, academic policies, and other university operations.

Associated Students of the University of California, Chancellor's Office, Student Environmental Resource Center, Zero Waste Coalition, Graduate Assembly, Office of Student Affairs, and the Housing and Dining division all appoint one student seat each on CACS and each have one vote.

At UC Berkeley, students participate in high-level committees and advisory boards that influence policy decisions.

UC Berkeley includes student representatives on its highest decision-making body, the UC Board of Regents. While the Board of Regents governs the entire University of California system rather than just the Berkeley campus, it serves as the ultimate authority over university policies, finances, and strategic planning across all UC campuses, including Berkeley.

 

 

 



Does at least one student representative have voting rights on the highest decision-making body?:
Yes


Description of other mechanisms used to consult students on institutional decisions, plans, or policies:

Each year, one UC student is appointed as a Student Regent with voting rights, representing all UC students on the Board. Additionally, a Student Regent-designate is selected to serve a year as a non-voting member before transitioning to the voting position. These Student Regents have the responsibility to advocate for student perspectives and engage with systemwide issues, bringing student concerns directly to the Regents’ decision-making process.

The Student Regent is a voting member of The Regents of the University of California, attending all meetings of the Board and its Committees and serving a one-year term. This position is not elected but is filled by application and open to all students currently enrolled in the UC system. From the time of appointment as a Student Regent, but prior to the commencement of service as a member of the Board, the appointee holds the title Regent-designate and is invited to participate in all meetings of the Board and of its Committees, but without a vote.

3.2 Academic staff participation in governance
Does the institution have one or more ongoing bodies through which academic staff can democratically participate in the governance of the institution?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL outlining the ongoing bodies through which academic staff can democratically participate in the governance of the institution:

UC Berkeley has several ongoing bodies that enable academic staff to participate in the governance of the institution. The primary body is the Berkeley Division of the Academic Senate, which is part of the University of California’s systemwide Academic Senate. This Senate provides faculty members with a structured and democratic way to engage in campus governance and decision-making.

Key elements of academic staff governance include:

Academic Senate Committees – Faculty members participate in numerous committees within the Academic Senate that influence a wide range of policies, from academic programs and faculty appointments to budget allocations and campus planning. Examples include the Committee on Educational Policy, the Committee on Research, and the Committee on Diversity, Equity, and Campus Climate.

Divisional Council (DIVCO) – This is the executive committee of the Berkeley Division of the Academic Senate. DIVCO represents the faculty in discussions with the administration and makes recommendations on policies affecting the campus. DIVCO members are elected by the faculty and serve as the voice of the broader academic community.

 



Are one or more academic staff representatives included as members of the institution’s highest decision-making body?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL outlining academic staff representation on the institution’s highest decision-making body:

UC Berkeley has ongoing bodies through which academic staff can democratically participate in the governance of the institution, with the primary example being the Academic Senate.

The Berkeley Division of the Academic Senate is part of the broader University of California Academic Senate, which serves as the primary governance body for faculty across the UC system. The Academic Senate allows academic staff to participate democratically in university governance through elections, committees, and representative councils.

UC faculty in the Academic Senate carry out shared-governance responsibilities established by The Regents. The Senate is empowered by UC's governing body, the Board of Regents, to exercise direct control over academic matters of central importance to the University – to determine academic policy, set conditions for admission and the granting of degrees, authorize and supervise courses and curricula; and to advise the administration on faculty appointments, promotions and budgets.

The Systemwide Academic Senate and the ten Divisional Senates (one for each UC campus) provide the organizational framework that enables the faculty to exercise its right to participate in the University's governance. The faculty voice is formed through a deliberative process that includes the Standing Committees of the Senate, the Academic Council, the Assembly of the Academic Senate, and their Divisional counterparts. Consultation with the senior administration occurs in a parallel structure: at the systemwide level between the Academic Council Chair and the President; and on the campus level between the Divisional Senate Chairs and the Chancellors. With some exceptions and as defined by the Standing Order of the Regents 105.1, Senate membership is granted to individuals who have a ladder-rank or other selected academic appointment at the University.

3.3 Non-academic staff participation in governance
Does the institution have one or more ongoing bodies through which non-academic staff can democratically participate in the governance of the institution?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL outlining the ongoing bodies through which non-academic staff can democratically participate in the governance of the institution:

UC Berkeley has ongoing bodies through which non-academic staff can participate in the governance of the institution. Key bodies through which non-academic staff can engage in governance include:

Council of UC Staff Assemblies (CUCSA) – This is a systemwide body that represents non-academic staff across all UC campuses, including Berkeley. CUCSA serves as the voice of non-academic staff to the UC Office of the President and the UC Board of Regents. It provides recommendations and works to ensure that the concerns and interests of non-academic staff are considered in university policies. CUCSA is made up of elected representatives from each campus, and UC Berkeley has staff members who participate in this body.

UC Berkeley Staff Assembly – The Staff Assembly is a campus-based organization that provides a forum for non-academic staff to engage in university governance and advocacy. It organizes programs, events, and initiatives designed to represent the interests of staff and encourage their involvement in decision-making processes. The Staff Assembly works with campus leadership on issues such as professional development, workplace policies, and staff welfare.

Staff Advisory Committees – UC Berkeley has various advisory committees that include non-academic staff representation. These committees provide input on issues such as diversity, equity, and inclusion; sustainability; and campus services. For example, the Chancellor’s Advisory Committee on Staff Diversity (CACSD) is one such body that works to improve staff diversity and inclusivity on campus.

Employee and Labor Relations Committees – Non-academic staff representatives can participate in committees focused on labor relations, including those dealing with union representation, working conditions, and compensation. UC Berkeley has staff members involved in these discussions, particularly in departments with unionized staff.

Non-academic staff at UC Berkeley can participate in the governance of the institution via advisory bodies of Chancellor’s Staff Advisory Committee (CSAC), The Chancellor’s Cabinet and other Advisory Commmitees on diversity, sustainability, staff development, and campus services.

3.4 Community participation in governance
Does the institution have one or more ongoing bodies through which local community-based organizations not affiliated with the institution can democratically participate in its governance?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL outlining the ongoing bodies through which local community-based organizations can democratically participate in the governance of the institution:

Chancellor's Community Advisory Board (CCAB) – This board serves as an advisory body for UC Berkeley, providing local community organizations with a platform to share concerns, offer input, and advise the Chancellor on matters related to community relations. The CCAB includes community leaders from diverse local organizations and functions as a bridge between the university and the surrounding community, especially on issues affecting neighborhood relations, social justice, and community engagement.

UC Berkeley’s Public Service Programs – UC Berkeley has numerous public service programs that partner with community-based organizations in the Bay Area. These programs facilitate collaboration between the university and local organizations. Examples include initiatives around sustainability, health, education, and social services, where local organizations often have a voice in shaping the programs.

Advisory Committees for Community Engagement – UC Berkeley also has various advisory committees and working groups that involve local community members, particularly in areas related to public health, sustainability, and social justice. These bodies provide community organizations with a forum to advise on university policies and initiatives that affect the local population.

Public Listening Sessions and Forums – UC Berkeley regularly holds public meetings and forums where community members, including representatives from local CBOs, can provide input on specific initiatives. These sessions are part of the university's ongoing efforts to maintain a strong relationship with its local community and ensure that its operations are aligned with local needs and values.

Berkeley Community Fund (BCF) – The Berkeley Community Fund is a nonprofit organization that supports local community-based organizations in the Berkeley area. It fosters collaboration between the university and local organizations. UC Berkeley has engaged with the BCF on various initiatives, particularly those related to community development and social equity.

 



Has the institution used one or more ad hoc mechanisms during the previous three years to consult local community members on institutional decisions, plans, or policies?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL outlining the ad hoc mechanisms used during the previous three years to consult local community members on institutional decisions, plans, or policies:

The Government and Community Relations unit of the Chancellor’s Office partners with the Berkeley City Manager’s Office to facilitate the meetings of the Berkeley City Council’s Special City/UC/Student Committee, which meets at least once each semester. Known as the 4x6 Committee, the special committee includes the (4) City Councilmembers who represent neighborhoods that are adjacent to the campus, (2) representatives of the undergraduate student government, (2) representatives of the graduate student government, and (2) campus administrators. The committee agendas and discussions focus on issues of mutual concern, including presentations on the university’s Capital Projects and university programs and initiatives that are of shared interest to the stakeholders.

UC Berkeley’s Government and Community Relations (GCR) website:

https://chancellor.berkeley.edu/gcr
