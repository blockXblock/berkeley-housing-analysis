4.1 Sustainable investment policy or committee
Has the institution formally incorporated environmental, social, and governance (ESG) factors and/or climate risk as material issues to be considered when making investment decisions?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL providing an overview of how the institution has incorporated ESG factors and/or climate risk as material issues to be considered when making investment decisions:

UC approaches sustainability, at every stage of the investment process, both from a risk management and investment opportunity perspective. UC believes that a focus on sustainability, including in particular climate change, food and water security and the circular economy, can help create better risk-adjusted returns over the long term. 

At the governance level, the UC Board of Regents’ Investment Policy Statement for the endowment makes clear that material ESG risks shall be considered with the same weight as any other material investment risk.  The Board of Regents’ policy regarding sustainability in endowment investments states in relevant part:

“The Office of the Chief Investment Officer shall incorporate environmental sustainability, social responsibility, and governance (ESG) into the investment evaluation process as part of its overall risk assessment in its investments decision making. ESG factors are considered with the same weight as other material risk factors influencing investment decision making.

The Office of the Chief Investment Officer uses a proprietary sustainability framework to provide core universal principles that inform the decisions and assist in the process of investment evaluation. The Office of the Chief 

Investment Officer manages the [endowment] consistent with these sustainability principles. The Framework can be found on the Office of the Chief Investment Officer website in the sustainability section.”

See: https://regents.universityofcalifornia.edu/policies/6102.pdf

At the Chief Investment Officer and staff level, all investment staff consider ESG factors in the investment process when such factors are material.  Our Framework for Sustainable Investing describes our views on material ESG factors in more detail.  See: https://www.ucop.edu/investment-office/sustainable-investment/sustainability-framework/index.html

To implement our approach, UC Investments has developed a range of practices.  We manage our ~$23 billion endowment through external managers.  The Director of ESG Integration conducts an ESG due diligence review of all potential new managers and investments, including in funds and co-investments.  The ESG review one of the considerations in whether to proceed with a new investment.

UC Investments oversees and monitors the firms that manage our endowment in order to ensure that material ESG issues, if any, are being adequately addressed.  Most of our external investment managers have best in class ESG staff and practices and are at the forefront of the investment industry’s efforts to integrate ESG considerations, including climate risk.

4.2 Negative screening and divestment
Does the institution employ negative screening and/or targeted divestment in support of sustainability?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL providing evidence of the institution’s use of negative screening and/or targeted divestment in support of sustainability:

UC Investments uses negative screening to manage material risk to the endowment.  We screen out the following from the endowment, across all asset classes (public equity, private equity, private credit, fixed income, real assets, real estate): fossil fuel, tobacco, private prisons, Sudan and firearms. 

To accomplish this negative screening, we require our external public equity investment managers to track the MSCI ACWI IMI ex Tobacco ex Fossil Fuel Index, which screens out roughly 300 publicly traded companies that own “proved and probable” reserves of thermal coal, oil and gas and tobacco-related companies.   We apply the same screening definition (i.e., any amount of fossil fuel reserves) to our other investments in public equities, fixed income and the private asset classes (private equity, absolute returns, real assets and real estate), thus comprehensively covering the entire endowment.  To the extent that fossil fuel reserve owning companies are held in commingled accounts, UC Investments continues to reduce exposure by, for example, converting such accounts into separately managed accounts that exclude fossil fuel reserve owning companies.

The MSCI ACWI IMI ex Tobacco ex-Fossil Fuel Index is available at:

https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/533e1509-9bc0-bdd4-946a-7736245c8c4e

Our screening of fossil fuel companies is summarized annually in our TCFD reports, available at: 

https://www.ucop.edu/investment-office/sustainable-investment/climate-change/index.html

4.3 Investor engagement 
Has the institution engaged in proxy voting in support of sustainability during the previous three years, either directly or through an investment manager?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL providing an overview of the institution’s proxy voting in support of sustainability:

UC Investments’ Proxy Voting Record: VDS Dashboard (issgovernance.com)

UC Investments votes its shares in accordance with custom proxy voting guidelines.  With limited exceptions, UC Investments does not delegate proxy voting to its managers.  In the rare instances in which managers vote our shares, we ask that they do so consistent with our proxy voting guidelines.  When they intend to vote otherwise, they contact us and we discuss the matter prior to the vote. 

Link to proxy voting guidelines: 

https://www.ucop.edu/investment-office/sustainable-investment/active-ownership/custom-proxy-guidelines.pdf

We make our proxy voting record available to the public through our website:

https://www.ucop.edu/investment-office/sustainable-investment/active-ownership/index.html

 



Has the institution filed a shareholder resolution or signed on to an investor statement in support of sustainability during the previous three years, either directly or through an investment manager?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL providing an overview of the shareholder resolutions or investor statements in support of sustainability:

UC Investments conducts extensive shareholder engagement in direct communication with the senior leadership of over 400 companies annually.  We believe this is a more effective tool for risk management than shareholder resolutions.  We can cover many more ESG risks and many more companies through direct engagement than shareholder resolutions.  Through our shareholder engagement, we engage in dialogue that leads to improved corporate approaches to material ESG risks, including climate change, water security and human capital.  

https://www.ucop.edu/investment-office/sustainable-investment/active-ownership/index.html

 



Is the institution an active participant in one or more sustainability or ESG-focused investor networks?:
Yes


Narrative and/or website URL providing an overview of the institution’s participation in sustainability or ESG-focused investor networks:

UC Investments participates in a number of investor networks to engage in policy advocacy and to share best practices, including, e.g., Climate Action 100+, FAIRR Collier, the UN Principles for Responsible Investment, the Intentional Endowments Network, the Council of Institutional Investors, the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures and the Thirty Percent Coalition.  These organizations regularly engage in advocacy with regulatory agencies to advance prudent sustainable investing frameworks and regulations on issues such as climate risk, corporate governance and antibiotic resistance in farm animals.

We are part of a ~$1.6 trillion investor network that engages on our behalf with senior leadership at ~400 companies per year - Federated Hermes/EOS.  Along with the other investors in this network, we focus on material ESG risks facing companies that we own.  For example in 2024, our endowment engaged directly with executive and board level leadership of more than 400 companies we own regarding material risks related to climate change and their risk mitigation strategies.

See: https://www.ucop.edu/investment-office/sustainable-investment/active-ownership/index.html;

https://www.ucop.edu/investment-office/sustainable-investment/climate-change/index.html
