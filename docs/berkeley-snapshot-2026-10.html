Water storage in the hills

EBMUD runs 170 distribution reservoirs and tanks across its system; it publishes no list by city. Inside Berkeley, mapped sites include Summit (416 Spruce St; EBMUD replaced the 37-million-gallon open reservoir with a 3.5-million-gallon tank in 2019), Berryman (2.6 million gallons, per its architect), Shasta, Woods and the covered Claremont reservoir: about six to eight sites, plus Berkeley View, named in the press as serving about 2,100 hill homes. The exact count and capacities await EBMUD confirmation.

A fire evacuation of the hills

Hours:minutes; the last vehicles take 3:25–3:40. A repeat of the 1923 fire: up to 4:10.

Plus 47,000 passing through as external traffic. 106 evacuation zones.
1,547
residential parcels with a single way out
139 neighborhoods; another 195 neighborhoods (3,715 parcels) have impaired secondary access.

How many streets jam? The City's study does not count them. It maps every congested road and names them: "One hour into the evacuation, the majority of the roads within the Evacuation Zones are operating at LOS F [demand over capacity] conditions including Grizzly Peak Blvd, Euclid Ave, Spruce St, Marin Ave, La Loma Ave, Gayley Rd, and Arlington Ave." The choke points are intersections, the Marin Avenue five-way roundabout among them, so red-curbing hill streets is "unlikely to substantially improve evacuation flow." Hill streets carry at most 900 cars an hour, and "severe congestion is expected to occur in all evacuation cases studied." Hills residents leaving early, combined with a faster departure, cut the time to clear 90% of vehicles by up to 68% (the last vehicles, by up to 14%).
