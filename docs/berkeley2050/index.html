Berkeley 2050

A citizen-expert initiative to give Berkeley what it never had: a 30-year plan for climate-smart, technologically advanced, resilient public infrastructure. Launched through the 2018 ballot (Measure R, approved by 84.6% of voters), carried by a volunteer task force of more than forty engineers, planners, scientists, and residents, advised from UC Berkeley and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, and the source of the City's adopted Vision 2050 Framework. This page is the initiative's history and complete document library.

Origins

Berkeley 2050 began in early 2017, in the months after voters passed the $100M Measure T1 infrastructure bond, when John Gage (co-founder of Sun Microsystems) and Gordon Wozniak (Berkeley City Councilmember for twelve years, District 8) concluded that setting T1's priorities had proven a larger point: a city rebuilding its infrastructure piecemeal, ballot measure by ballot measure, needed instead a single long-range plan — looking to the year 2050 — grounded in engineering, finance, and the technologies arriving along the way.

The documented founding sequence, from the founders' archive:

  • February 24, 2017 — Gage & Wozniak complete the first full deck, "Infrastructure and Climate Change" (45 slides): drought, flooding, the >$1 billion needs figure, bond-capacity analysis.
  • March 7, 2017 — they present "Infrastructure2050" to the Berkeley City Council. The closing slide asks the Council to adopt a goal for "Infrastructure2050 — modern, durable, green, sustainable," and to "Develop Vision for Infrastructure2050 — UC Berkeley, Berkeley Lab; establish planning process; citizen involvement" — the name, the university partnership, and the citizen task force, all in one slide.
  • March 21, 2017 — the companion presentation to EBMUD on water, sewer, and revenue-bond financing.
  • April 12, 2017 — Gage writes to Eric Schmidt (then Executive Chairman of Alphabet, who replied the same week) describing the wider campaign: a $2 billion "Berkeley2050 City Resilience Plan" uniting campus and city infrastructure, Wozniak's financing design (aggregate the City's revenue streams to back long-term bonds), and early support from Mayor Jesse Arreguín, the new City Council, UC Chancellor Carol Christ, UC CFO Nathan Brostrom, and Prof. Robert Reich.
  • August 24, 2017 — the founders circulate two companion documents. The handout carries the language that would become the Framework's: America's D+ infrastructure grade, the WPA legacy, "close to $1 billion in infrastructure needs," and a section titled "Where we want to be in 2050." And "How would we build Berkeley if we started today?" takes the outline of global warming from Paul Hawken's book Drawdown — its ranking of climate solutions by gigatons of carbon impact — and converts each solution into a "Berkeley Measure": the city data to gather, the technology inventories to build, and the near- and long-term actions, costed with life-cycle methods from UC Berkeley's Arpad Horvath and the Berkeley Water Center. Climate action wasn't appended to the infrastructure plan; it was the organizing framework from the first year.

The city takes it up. Mayor Arreguín announced the initiative in his 2017 State of the City address as an intention "to develop Vision 2050, a long-term infrastructure plan." On July 31, 2018 the City Council voted unanimously to place Measure R on the November ballot, asking voters:

"Shall the measure, advising the Mayor to engage citizens and experts in the development of Vision 2050, a 30-year plan to identify and guide implementation of climate-smart, technologically-advanced, integrated and efficient infrastructure to support a safe, vibrant and resilient future for Berkeley, be adopted?" Measure R, November 6, 2018 — approved 44,177 to 8,014 (84.64%)

A task force of more than forty volunteers had begun work that spring, organized into four work groups — quality of life, environment, technology, and finance/management — holding public information sessions through fall 2018 and community workshops through summer 2019. The result, the Vision 2050 Framework, was delivered in May 2020 and adopted by the City Council on September 29, 2020.

The Framework

Adopted by the City Council on September 29, 2020 — "not a strategic plan… in essence, it is a call to action."

Three guiding principles

  • Support vibrant and safe communities
  • Have efficient, inspired and well-maintained infrastructure
  • Facilitate a green Berkeley and contribute to saving our planet

Five strategies

  • Ensure integrated and balanced planning (multi-criteria decisions; "Dig Once")
  • Manage infrastructure cradle to grave (master planning; asset management)
  • Adopt sustainable and safe technologies (electrification, complete streets, sensors, wildfire mitigation)
  • Invest in our future (double capital spending against the unfunded liability)
  • Prepare the City's organization to deliver a major capital program, with independent oversight

The numbers that drove it

  • $837M of capital assets, half past useful life (2020)
  • $882M unfunded infrastructure liability (2020) — over $2 billion by 2025
  • Capital spending need: ~$80M/yr — roughly double the then-current rate
  • Bonding capacity to ~$1B by 2050 at peer-city debt-service rates

Civic leadership & recognition

"That is why we called this effort Vision 2050. How do we create a plan to invest and re-envision our city by the year 2050?… By 2050, we want to design and create a public realm that is sustainable, resilient and equitable." Mayor Jesse Arreguín, foreword to the Vision 2050 Framework (2020)

Mayor Arreguín championed the initiative from his 2017 State of the City address onward, signed the official ballot argument for Measure R (with Councilmember Susan Wengraf, the Sierra Club's Luis Amezcua, and Gordon Wozniak), and introduced the adopted Framework in 2020. During those years Arreguín served simultaneously as President of ABAG — the Association of Bay Area Governments — and the Framework aligns Berkeley's infrastructure planning with ABAG's regional Plan Bay Area 2050.

In 2025, Mayor Adena Ishii reconvened the task force, whose "Realize Vision 2050" recommendations — plan first, maintain cradle-to-grave, borrow in a disciplined series, oversee independently — now anchor Berkeley's infrastructure debate.
