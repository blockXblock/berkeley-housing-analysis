Why the comparison starts in 2018

Because that is where the City's building-by-building record starts. We compare against the City's Annual Progress Reports to the state, and the state's open-data copy holds Berkeley's project-level rows for 2018–2025. Each row names a project and the year it was entitled, permitted and completed. That is what makes a building-by-building comparison possible.

The start date comes from state law. Two 2017 housing laws, Assembly Bill 879 and Senate Bill 35, changed what cities must report. The state housing department (HCD) rewrote the report form to match, and its guidance draws the line at 2018: “For the years 2017 and prior, you must use the old version of the APR.” Berkeley's rows in the state's open data begin with 2018, the first year on the new form. (HCD report instructions; HCD FAQ.)

Our permit records were requested to match. The City's permit reports, obtained through public-records requests, cover permits from 2018 onward.

What did not set the date. Berkeley's Measure O housing bond and Measure P transfer tax both passed in 2018, but their money reached projects from 2019 on, so 2018 is simply the year before the new local money. The current state housing-target cycle (RHNA) starts in mid-2022 and counts building permits, not completions. Some of our other records reach further back (state tax-credit awards to 1991; we are collecting City planning records back to 2006), but without the City's project-level filings there is nothing to compare them against.

How to check us

Every count traces to a primary document: the permit's own text, the building's plan set, the City's staff reports. The City's report is what we check against, never what we count from. Every correction is a row in a public ledger (195 entries, each with its evidence), every finding is a row in audit_findings.csv, and every change appends a new dated baseline; nothing is silently edited. The pipeline rebuilds from the raw permit files:

JN-A (ingest) → JN-B (dedup) → JN-C (classify) → JN-F (corrections) → buildings → JN-E (reconcile)

The notebooks, ledgers, baselines and audit memos, including our retractions, are in the public repository. The curriculum teaches the method for any city.
