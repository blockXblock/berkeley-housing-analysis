# More homes, healthy corridors — what Berkeley's records say works
*A one-page data briefing · July 2026 · berkeleybuild.com — every figure traces to a public record;
method audited at berkeleybuild.com/housing-audit.html (our independent count vs the city's state
filing: 130 units apart out of ~4,200, every row of difference named in both directions).*

## What works
- **Ministerial speed is real.** ADU permits: **median 3 days** to issuance (2,881 permits since
  2018). Solar: 1,076 permits in 13 months. The new **Middle Housing certificate: 15 approved,
  0 denied**, approvals inside ~2.5 months of filing.
- **Middle Housing year one is small and calm**: 28 applications; two-thirds are additions to
  existing homes; ~15–20 net new units proposed. Neither a flood nor a failure — today it's a
  homeowner-flexibility tool. SB 9: zero applications.

## Where time dies
- New-housing permits wait a **median 189 days** for issuance; the 90th percentile waits
  **527 days**; the slowest tenth of completed projects take **3.5+ years** application-to-final.
  One 63-unit building waited **4.9 years** for its permit.
- Berkeley rarely says no (**743 approved vs 52 denied** planning decisions in 13 months) — it
  says *wait*. 539 applications sit pending right now. **Delay, not denial, is the policy variable.**

## Where the homes come from — and the corridors
- **44% of all units since 2018 came from 10% of projects** — the corridor buildings (Shattuck
  695u, San Pablo 397u, University 327u, Telegraph 271u). ADUs and Middle Housing add the rest in
  residential fabric, **touching zero storefronts**.
- **No housing-kills-business pattern**: the corridors with the most new housing have the fullest
  storefronts (Shattuck/University/Telegraph ~19–21% of commercial parcels without an active
  license vs San Pablo 34%; Solano 6%). The real weak spot is **ground floors of new buildings**
  (preliminary: 2 of the 14 largest corridor completions show an active business license at their
  address — field verification in progress). Lever: tenanting/vacancy activation, not restriction.

## The money (pays once at the counter, then every year)
- **~$250M/year** of construction permitted (declared valuation). Fees ≈ $4–7M/yr *(modeled at an
  illustrative 2%; actuals requested via CPRA)*.
- 40+-unit completions since 2018 added **$857M of assessed improvements → ≥$8.6M/yr** at the 1%
  base levy alone (one building, 1951 Shattuck: ~$704k/yr). Fiscally a tower ≈ a thousand ADUs;
  ADUs add homes at near-zero fiscal and commercial footprint. Nonprofit projects are tax-exempt —
  **affordable housing pays in homes, not taxes**. The portfolio beats any single-lane story.

## Oakland already shows what Berkeley makes people ask for
Tested live this week, same Accela software both cities: an Oakland homeowner or developer can
**download a full month of permit records in one click** (2,351 records, June 2026, with
descriptions) and see **where every application sits** — *Pending–Incomplete, Intake–Completed,
In Review* — the statutory-clock states, public. Berkeley shows 10 records a page, statuses
reduced to *Issued/Finaled*, and the clock states only via records request. Openness is a subsidy
that costs nothing: applicants who can see the queue plan with confidence. And Oakland hides its
milestone *dates* — so Berkeley can leapfrog, not just catch up.

## Four asks that cost nothing
1. **Publish the building-permit feed** — with dates and clock states — on the city's own
   open-data portal (it publishes business licenses, but no permits; one move leapfrogs Oakland;
   the internal report used for records requests captures **40–50% fewer filings** than the
   public portal shows).
2. **Publish deemed-complete dates** — one field makes every statutory-clock question answerable.
3. **Publish the permit fee ledger** — replace models with actuals.
4. **Fix three broken open datasets** found this week: zoning (no attributes), budget (expenses
   only, ends FY2016), and the permit-report filters.

*The record watches itself: the state filing is re-pulled and diffed on schedule with five
anchor-validated error detectors; licenses snapshot monthly; permits refresh by harvest and CPRA.
Independent reconstruction from primary sources only — the city's filings are our verification
target, never our source.*
