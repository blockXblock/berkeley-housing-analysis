The streets, by class

The corridors that carry 48% of the new housing are the 22 arterial miles — one-tenth of the street network. Under it all: the sewers are mostly 6-inch vitrified-clay pipe (mains run 6–18 inches), and the city has rehabilitated 227 of the 254 miles since 1986 — the pipes are older than every building on the deck.

The sealed surface

Berkeley is 6,156 acres. The city's own watershed plan counts 848 acres of streets + 182 acres of sidewalks — 17% of the city is public pavement before a single roof or parking lot is counted. Where the city has measured whole watersheds, imperviousness runs 34% (Gilman, near the hills) to 55% (Potter, the flats) — rain that once soaked in now runs to the Bay through the storm drains.

Streets: City of Berkeley 2022 Pavement Management Program update (P-TAP 23 final report) — 214.16 centerline / 448.73 lane-miles; 39.63M sq ft of pavement; PCI 55. Sewers: City SSMP — ~254 miles, 7,200 manholes, ~31,600 laterals; 6″ VCP is the priority/workhorse pipe; 227 miles rehabilitated since 1986. Two streams: the metronome and the towers

The same 4,229 completed homes, split by building size instead of street: ■ buildings over 4 units delivered 3,382 units on just 53 permits; ■ small projects (1–4 units, mostly ADUs) delivered 847 on 792. The small stream is a metronome — 67–135 units every single year, through COVID, rate spikes, everything — while the tower stream swings 4× with the financing cycle. Policy reads: the ministerial lane produces reliability; the big buildings produce volume.

Our total units completed match the city's official numbers almost exactly (ours | city's state filing) — 2022: 836 | 828 · 2023: 720 | 716 · 2024: 715 | 708. In 2025 — 600 | 492 — most of the gap is two completed group-living buildings the city's filing omits (80 units: a 41-unit project on San Pablo and the 39-unit 1367 University), plus a tail of small ADU completions. 5 · Housing did not hurt the corridors — vacancy is a separate problem

  • The corridors with the most new housing (Shattuck, University, Telegraph) sit at the healthiest end of the business-license table (19–21% of commercial parcels without an active license, vs 34% on San Pablo and 6% on Solano — a 5× spread unrelated to where housing was built).
  • The genuine weak spot: ground floors of new buildings. Preliminary: only 2 of the 14 largest corridor completions show an active non-rental business license at their street addresspreliminary — field check in progress
  • Policy lever: tenanting support and vacancy activation, not housing restriction. The city already taxes empty homes (Measure M); empty storefronts aren't even measured.
Day-3 overlay: Nov-2025 license roll × assessor commercial parcels (institutional parcels inflate some corridor shares; single-snapshot caveat applies — monthly license snapshots now running).

7 · Oakland already shows the public what Berkeley makes them ask for

We tested both cities' permit systems this week — same software (Accela), same county, very different windows. This is what a homeowner or developer sees without filing a single records request:

OaklandBerkeley
Bulk export of permit records One click — a full month (2,351 records, with descriptions) downloads instantly None — 10 records per page; a full pull requires scraping or a CPRA
Where an application sits Full lifecycle public: Pending–Incomplete, Intake–Completed, In Review, On Hold — the statutory-clock states Terse Issued / Finaled; the clock states exist in the system but aren't shown
What kind of project it is Pre-classified record types (1–2 unit vs 3+ residential vs commercial; New vs Alteration; SolarApp+) Generic "Building Permit"

Why it matters for housing: an applicant who can see the queue plans with confidence — openness is a subsidy that costs nothing. And Berkeley can leapfrog: Oakland hides its milestone dates too. Publish the permit feed with dates and clock states and Berkeley becomes the East Bay's transparency benchmark, not its laggard.

Live probes 2026-07-03: Oakland ACA Download-results specimen (June 2026, 2,351 records, committed); Berkeley ACA pagination behavior; both cities' open-data portals lack any permits dataset.

8 · Five asks that cost almost nothing and change everything

  • Write open data into the Clariti contract. The city's new ~$5 million permit-system contract (Clariti, replacing Accela for permit pipeline data management) must require a public open-data API. With it, independent verification like ours — and every journalist's, student's, and neighbor's — updates in minutes instead of months of records requests and scraping. A data-access clause costs nothing at signing and is nearly impossible to add later.
  • Publish the building-permit feed — with dates and clock states — on the open-data portal the city already runs: it publishes business licenses today, but not a single permit dataset, and that one move leapfrogs Oakland. (Our CPRA comparison found the internal report used for data requests captures 40–50% fewer filings than the public portal shows — undocumented filters.)
  • Publish deemed-complete dates — one field, and every statutory-clock question (the ADU 60-day rule, AB 2234) becomes answerable instead of arguable. The data already leaves the building: we found the current Accela portal sends the FULL processing timeline — deemed-complete, CEQA, decision, appeal dates — to every visitor's browser, hidden in the page code (display:none). Showing it is a display setting, not a data project.
  • Publish the fee ledger — replace modeled fee figures with actuals.
  • Fix the three broken open datasets we hit this week: zoning (geometry, zero attributes), budget (expenses only, ends FY2016), and the permit-report filters above.
Each verified against the live portal and Accela; CPRA requests for the fee/deemed-complete items are filed/in progress. Hidden-timeline finding: Processing Status detail pages, verified 2026-07-10 (ZP2022-0170 et al. — the workflow table ships in hidden DOM rows).

9 · This doesn't stop when the meeting ends

The record that produced these slides watches itself: the state filing is re-pulled and diffed on schedule (five mechanical error-detectors, anchor-validated), business licenses snapshot monthly, and the permit stream refreshes by harvest and CPRA. When the city's numbers move, we know the week it happens — and every claim keeps its receipt.
