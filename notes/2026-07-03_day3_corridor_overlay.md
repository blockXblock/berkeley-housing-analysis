# Day 3 — Corridor / business overlay (mayor-prep)

Method: street-name corridor classification (the 14 named commercial arterials — SHATTUCK,
UNIVERSITY, SAN PABLO, TELEGRAPH, ADELINE, COLLEGE, SOLANO, MLK, GILMAN, HOPKINS, DWIGHT, ASHBY,
EUCLID, HEARST); transparent and checkable. The city's zoning-districts open dataset is a shell
(geometry, zero attributes) — noted as another portal-quality exhibit. Sources: v2
`v_projects_flat` (completions 2018+), `berkeley.db` parcels (UseCode 3x/7x = commercial-class)
and the Nov-2025 license roll. License↔parcel joins on the 10-digit APN prefix (book+page+parcel)
because big mixed-use buildings condo-ize: the ground-floor retail unit carries a sub-APN distinct
from the project parcel (1500 San Pablo specimen: project …00900, tenant license …00205).

## Exhibit A — where the housing went

**44% of all completed units since 2018 came from the 10% of projects on corridors** (69 corridor
projects, 1,949 units vs 635 fabric projects, 2,516 units). By corridor: Shattuck 695u ·
San Pablo 397u · University 327u · Telegraph 271u · Dwight 125u · Ashby 94u · rest ≤21u.
**Deck message:** corridor towers carry the unit totals; the residential-fabric stream (ADUs,
Middle Housing) is many hands, small numbers — and touches zero storefronts.

## Exhibit B — ground-floor tenancy of the big corridor buildings (a LEAD, not a verdict)

Of the **14 largest corridor completions (2019–2026), only 2 have an active non-rental business
license at their street number** (Teak Me Home at 1500 San Pablo; one at 2527 San Pablo). Several
lack even a landlord rental license at that number.
**Caveats before quoting:** (1) corner-lot retail addressed on the cross street is missed — the
PERMANENT address-assignment rule (1951 Shattuck IS the County's "2108 Berkeley Way" corner-lot
specimen); (2) some buildings may have no retail space at all; (3) the roll is Nov-2025 (newest
completions haven't had time). **Follow-up queued:** CIC cross-street spot-check of each of the 14
ground floors before the deck states a number. Even discounted, the direction supports the deck's
commercial-zone message: the risk to local business isn't housing construction — it's ground
floors that sit empty after completion.

## Exhibit C — corridor commercial parcels with no active license (vacancy proxy)

962 corridor commercial-class parcels; **~29% show no active business license at the parcel
prefix.** Ranking (share with no license): Euclid 50% · Hearst 48% · Hopkins 47% · College 38% ·
San Pablo 34% · Ashby 31% · Dwight 26% · Adeline 25% · Telegraph 21% · University 20% · Shattuck
19% · Gilman 14% · **Solano 6%**.
**Caveats:** UseCode 3x/7x includes institutional parcels that legitimately hold no business
license (Euclid/Hearst are campus-edge — their highs are partly institutional, not vacancy);
single-snapshot roll (renewal flicker, the Amoeba lesson) means parcel-level absence is a
suspect, not a verdict. **The defensible read is the CONTRAST:** Solano at 6% vs San Pablo at 34%
— corridor business health varies 5× across town, independent of where housing got built.

## Deck synthesis (the commercial-zone answer)

1. Housing production concentrates on a few corridors (A) — and the corridors with the most new
   housing (Shattuck, University, Telegraph) sit at the *healthy* end of the license table (C).
   The data shows no housing-kills-business pattern; if anything the busiest housing corridors
   have the fullest storefronts.
2. The genuine commercial-zone problem the data surfaces is **post-completion ground-floor
   vacancy in new buildings** (B) — a tenanting problem, not a construction problem. Policy
   levers: ground-floor tenancy support/vacancy activation, not housing restriction.
3. The fabric stream (ADU ~20/mo, Middle Housing ~3.5/mo) adds homes with zero storefront
   contact — the "more housing, no commercial impact" lane is already running (Day 2).
