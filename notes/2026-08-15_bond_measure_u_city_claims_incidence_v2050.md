### Analysis

**C1/C2 (the advertised rate) — arithmetically defensible, rhetorically layered.** The voter-facing label says $22.14/$100k. The peak $35 appears only in the impartial analysis/TRS; the combined-with-existing $44.13 only in a staff report; the map-relevant fact — that the peak-period debt service (~$19.5M/yr) levied on *today's* $29.0B base would be **$67/$100k** — appears nowhere. The $22.14 "average" is low for three stacked reasons: (a) it averages over 40 years including early years when only $100M is outstanding; (b) it averages over a growing base — the implied average base behind $15.25M/yr ÷ $22.14 is **$68.9B, 2.37× today's**; (c) "per $100k of assessed value" reads as neutral but AV varies ~15× across physically similar houses (see §2). None of this is false; all of it systematically understates what any *identifiable current voter* will pay.

**The base-growth subsidy (the deepest point).** The city's own $35 peak in 2040-41 implies the base grows ~4.8%/yr (to ~$56B by 2040); the 2020 Vision 2050 report itself assumed 5.5%/yr AV growth. Under Prop 13, non-selling parcels grow at most 2%/yr — so **~⅔ of the base growth that delivers the advertised low rate must come from reassessment-at-sale and new construction**. The advertised $22 average is funded by future buyers and future buildings. The same mechanism that makes the rate look small is the mechanism that concentrates payment on newcomers.

**C3 — interest exceeds principal.** $610M total service on $300M borrowed, final collection FY2066/67. A child born this year pays this tax until age 40. Not mentioned in the label.

**C5 (independent oversight) — overstated.** The substance (measure §6): an annual City Manager report, annual reports from two existing council-appointed commissions (Parks/Rec/Waterfront; Transportation & Infrastructure), and a City Auditor audit "at least once every three years." There is no dedicated independent citizens' bond oversight committee. "Independent" in the label is carrying more weight than the text supports.

**C6/C14 (what it funds) — the label names sympathetic categories; the text authorizes nearly anything.** Legally eligible: any "acquisition or improvement of real property for public infrastructure and facilities," explicitly including projects never surfaced in the 2026 public process; the project list is non-binding; proceeds can reimburse money already spent. Voters are approving an envelope, not a program.

**C7 (Vision 2050) — invoked, not implemented.** See §3. The impartial analysis tells voters the bonds finance projects "documented in... the Vision 2050 Framework"; the Vision 2050 Task Force's own 2025 sequence (program plan → prioritization → funding ask) was not followed, and the city's own materials say project-by-project prioritization "will happen after voters approve funding."

**C8 (the need) — the number moves.** $882M (2020) → >$1B (Dec 2025) → >$1.5B (adopted CIP) → "$1.65–2B" (webpage) → >$2.1B (proposed CIP). A 2.4× spread across the city's own documents is itself evidence that no stable program plan sits under the ask.

**C9/C10 (comparisons) — favorable baselines.** "Less than the late-1990s rate" compares against the era when Berkeley was simultaneously servicing the 1992/1996 bond stack. The Oakland/Albany comparison is a cumulative-tax point estimate with no base disclosed. Both are fair campaign material and both invite the response: the relevant comparison is against the $2B need and the plan (absent) to meet it, not against neighbors' tax bills.

**C11 (urgency) — true, and double-edged.** Construction escalation (~4%/yr per V2050 2020) is real; two years' delay costs ~$25–40M of buying power. But the same escalation logic applies to the ~$1.7B of need this bond *doesn't* fund; urgency argues for a complete financing plan at least as strongly as for this particular $300M.

**C13 — 13% overhead, and a fiscal-relief undercurrent.** $40.5M of the $313M program is staffing/implementation. Combined with the reimbursement clause (C14) and the findings' own citation of the $31M/$29M General Fund deficits, the bond functions partly as balance-sheet relief for a city in deficit — legal, but not what the label advertises.

**C15 — the measure is below its threshold in the city's own poll.** 64% post-arguments vs 66.7% required. This is why credible opposition (or conditional neutrality) has leverage.

## 2. Who pays — the cost structure under the ad-valorem levy

All figures from `databases/berkeley.db` (Feb-2026 assessor, 27,618 parcels with AV>0, $29.01B total) at the map's benchmark: full $300M serviced at 5%/30yr = $19.5M/yr → **$67.3 per $100k AV** on today's base. (City's phased plan reaches the same ~$19.5M/yr at peak; its lower quoted rates come entirely from levying on a projected larger future base — see §1. The cross-sectional *distribution* below is rate-invariant: at the city's $35 peak rate every figure scales by 0.52.)

- **Concentration:** top 1% of parcels (276) pay **20.3%** of the bond; top 5% pay 32.6%; top 10% pay **42.3%**; the bottom half of all parcels pays **14.9%**.
- **Deciles:** median annual cost runs $53 (decile 1) → $1,610 (decile 10); the top decile alone carries 42.3%.
- **Single-family spread (n=17,297):** p10 $75/yr, median $449, p90 $1,159, p99 $2,127 — a **15.4× p90/p10 spread** for the identical bond on similar houses. 18.7% of SFRs are assessed under $200k (< $135/yr); the 15.1% assessed over $1.5M — overwhelmingly recent purchases — pay > $1,009/yr.
- **At the city's peak rate ($35/$100k, 2040-41):** median SFR $233/yr; recent buyer at $1.5M $525/yr; p10 long-held SFR $39/yr. Add existing GO debt (~$22–31/$100k) per the city's own $44.13 combined figure.
- **Apartments (UseCode 7xxx, n=3,951):** $6.47B AV → **22.3% of the bond, $4.35M/yr**, largely renter-borne via pass-through.
- **New housing pays the most.** The city's top payers are recent apartment buildings. Joining v2 pipeline completions (5+ units, CO ≥ 2018) to the assessor via `to_canonical_apn`: **33 matched projects, 2,237 units, $1.02B AV → ~$690k/yr ≈ 3.5% of the entire bond** from ~0.1% of parcels; ~$306/unit/yr. This **undercounts**: 9 projects (736 units) didn't match on stale/re-platted APNs — including 2352 Shattuck (237u, CO 2022), which the assessor shows at $117.9M AV ≈ **$79k/yr**, a top-5 payer citywide. 2580 Bancroft (117u) ≈ $105k/yr; 1500 San Pablo (159u) ≈ $67k/yr.
- **The one-sentence incidence finding:** *an ad-valorem bond is paid disproportionately by exactly who arrived most recently — new buyers and new buildings — while the longest-tenured owners, whose tenure spans the decades the infrastructure was allowed to decay, pay the least.* And per §1, the city's low advertised average rate is itself financed by that same future-newcomer base growth.

**Honesty rails (carry into any published use):** AV ≠ market value ≠ wealth (the point, not a bug — but the AV distribution is not a wealth distribution); homeowner/nonprofit/veteran exemptions not modeled; the $67 figure is the today's-base benchmark, not the city's projected nominal rate — always present the reconciliation (same debt service, different base), or the campaign will correctly say the map "triples" the official rate. Recorded-document recency ≠ tenure (refi contamination; retracted 2026-08-14). **Map TODO:** add a "city assumptions" mode ($35 peak / $22.14 avg) alongside the today's-base mode, with the base-growth explanation — pre-empts the rebuttal and makes the base-growth subsidy visible.

## 3. The bond vs Vision 2050's official recommendations

### Where the bond genuinely aligns
- **Phased issuance:** $100M every 5 years is a *series*, not a lump — closer to V2050's sequencing than critics sometimes allow.
- **Categories:** fire/emergency, parks/waterfront, civic buildings, accessibility all appear in V2050's asset categories; staff say candidate projects were screened using "criteria recommended by the Vision 2050 framework" (Envision 60% / community input 40%).
- **All-electric requirement** aligns with V2050 Strategy 3 (clean energy/electrification).
- **Oversight/reporting nod** to V2050 Strategy 5 ("independent oversight and reporting") — though see C5.

### Where it inverts Vision 2050
| Vision 2050 (2020 + Oct 2025 official reports) | Measure U |
|---|---|
| **B: Infrastructure Program Plan by Spring 2026**, "to help the City Council discuss the timing and what should be included in a future funding measure" — plan first, ask second | No program plan adopted; prioritization done only at asset-category level; project-by-project prioritization deferred until "**after voters approve funding**" |
| **$250–300M in a series of GO bonds over 25–30 years** "without breaching responsible debt thresholds" | The **entire envelope authorized at once**, issued over ~10 years (2027–2037), $610M total debt service to FY2066/67 — no headroom left for the sequenced, plan-driven bonds V2050 described |
| **C: Asset Management Program** operating first, so new assets don't decay like the old (AMP for facilities/paving/sidewalks/sewers by July 2027) | AMP in progress (NexGen) but not complete; measure has only a hortatory maintenance "whereas" |
| **D: "discuss public polling and funding options for a potential funding measure in 2026 *or 2028*"** weighing "readiness and prioritization of the work... the economy, other funding measures that will be on the ballot"; 2025 report flags "a sense of the public feeling tax fatigue" and the Measure L failure | 2026 chosen with 7 measures on the ballot incl. a companion sales tax; city's own poll: 64% < 66.7% |
| Whole-need coverage: streets (PCI 57, "at risk"), undergrounding along evacuation routes, green/stormwater infrastructure (Watershed Plan $207M), transfer station ("exceeds serviceable life"), urban forest | **Absent** from the project list (streets covered separately by Measure FF). Fire gets $106.7M vs a ~$350M master-plan need; Civic Center seismic gets partial-scope line items ($3.6M Maudelle Shirek, $1.0M Veterans vs ~$13.9M full seismic each) that don't return the buildings to use |
| A: Unified cross-department approach; UC Berkeley / LBNL / UC Davis collaboration for technology | Not reflected in the measure; no mechanism |

### Verdict for the op-ed frame
The measure **cites Vision 2050 in its findings and the impartial analysis while inverting its central sequence** (plan → prioritize → maintain → then ask), **consumes the Task Force's entire stated 25–30-year debt envelope in one authorization**, and **funds partial scopes across categories while leaving whole V2050 categories unaddressed**. The strongest, most defensible line remains fiscal: *this single measure spends the whole responsible borrowing capacity identified by the city's own task force, without the program plan that task force said must come first* — backed by §2's incidence facts about who is asked to pay it.

## 4. The lifecycle sort (Larry Henry's question, 2026-08-15) — v0 from the city's own project list

LH's organizing question: *where is the lifecycle cost analysis?* Bond/tax proceeds build; they do not maintain. Sort the 36-project list three ways: (A) durable investment, long design life, low annual carry; (B) catch-up on renewal/maintenance of existing assets — legitimate, but not new capacity; (C) new or expanded facilities arriving with a **permanent operating obligation and no identified revenue**. The city has published **no per-project O&M estimate or design life anywhere** (not in the measure, the staff reports, or the project list), so this v0 sort is our judgment from the city's own project descriptions — every boundary call flagged. That the sort *requires* judgment is itself the finding: the analysis Vision 2050 existed to insist on was never done.

**A. Durable / long-life / low-carry ≈ $42M ≈ 15%** — South Cove Seawall ($5.7M), Marina timber piles ($3.0M), Marina Blvd sea-level-rise + Bay Trail segments ($1.9M + $5.25M + $2.28M, partial funding), 50/50 sidewalks ($14.6M), ADA barrier removal ($4.5M), Monkey Island/Garber paths ($3.3M), medians ($1.6M). Notably: the categories V2050 ranked as core resilient infrastructure (storm drains, green infrastructure, undergrounding) are **absent entirely** — the durable tier is thin because the durable categories aren't in the measure.

**B. Catch-up renewal of existing assets ≈ $150M ≈ 55%** — King Pool ($5.0M), park restrooms (~$3.2M), play structures ($2.65M), Glendale La Loma ($3.9M), Harrison turf conversion ($6.6M — ⚠ ~8–10 yr design life on 40-yr money), animal services ($3.65M), senior center ($3.6M), Center St elevators ($8.3M), 2180 Milvia end-of-life MEP ($11.7M), 2100 MLK public-safety/911 modernization ($18.4M), Old City Hall + Veterans seismic partials ($4.6M — don't return buildings to use), Fire Stations 4 & 6 replacements ($76.5M — replacement of existing function; expansion component flagged to C).

**C. New/expanded capacity → permanent new O&M, no identified revenue ≈ $82M ≈ 30%** — Frances Albrier replacement ($36.85M — "significantly undersized" → bigger facility, bigger carry), Adeline corridor open space ($6.0M new parkland), Dwight/Telegraph open-space expansion ($3.5M), 2636 MLK behavioral-health building ($3.39M city share of new two-story facility), African American Holistic Resource Center gap ($1.5M), Fire Training Center ($30.1M purpose-built new), new permanent waterfront restroom ($0.79M).

**The lifecycle mismatch datum:** final bond collection is **FY2066/67**. Artificial turf (~10 yr), play structures (~15–20 yr), HVAC/MEP (~20–25 yr), elevators (~25 yr) — a meaningful slice of Category B assets will be worn out and replaced (possibly twice) while taxpayers are still servicing the debt that bought them. This is precisely the cradle-to-grave violation V2050's Strategy 2 was written to prevent.

**LH's five questions — what we can already answer:**
1. *Per-project O&M + design life:* **published nowhere.** This is the ask.
2. *Total new O&M obligation + funding source:* not stated anywhere; the only carry figure in the record is $40.5M of bond-funded *delivery* staffing (not O&M). New O&M lands on a General Fund already projected $31M/$29M in deficit — the measure's own findings cite those deficits.
3. *T1 delivered vs promised:* per the 2025 V2050 report — Phase 1 (2017-22) spent $42.7M on 40 projects ($65.7M with grants); Phase 2 $60M/30 projects; "Measure T1 funding is almost exhausted." Existing GO carrying cost: ~$31/$100k avg over next 30 yrs (Dec 2025 work session).
4. *Does U follow or precede the capital plan:* **precedes it, definitively** — V2050's Spring-2026 Program Plan was not adopted; the city's own materials defer project-by-project prioritization until "after voters approve funding."
5. *Median-household cost, plainly:* median SFR (AV $667k): **~$148/yr at the advertised average rate, ~$233/yr at the disclosed peak** (2040-41) — for Measure U alone; existing GO adds ~$31/$100k (~$207/yr median); Measure V sales tax adds ~$9–10M/yr citywide on top. A *recent buyer* at $1.5M AV: ~$332 avg / ~$525 peak for U alone.

### Discrepancy inventory (useful for rebuttal work)
1. Unfunded need: $882M → >$1B → >$1.5B → $1.65–2B → >$2.1B across city documents (2020–2026).
2. "Independent oversight" (label) vs two council-appointed commissions + triennial audit (text).
3. "$22.14 avg" (label) vs $35 peak (TRS) vs $44.13 combined (staff report) vs $67/$100k today's-base equivalent (assessor math).
4. "Guided by" the public project list (findings) vs unlisted projects eligible + reimbursement of prior spending (text).
5. V2050 named in findings vs V2050's Spring-2026 program-plan precondition unmet, prioritization deferred to post-passage.
