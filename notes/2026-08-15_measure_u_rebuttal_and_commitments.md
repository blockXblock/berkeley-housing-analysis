## A. Draft rebuttal to the argument against Measure U (≈240 words)

> **REBUTTAL TO ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE U**
>
> Opponents say Berkeley has no plan. Here is the plan — and the guarantees.
>
> Every Measure U dollar is restricted by law to public infrastructure. Spending is audited
> by Berkeley's independent elected City Auditor and reported publicly every year.
>
> And the City Council has committed, by resolution, to go further **before major bonds are
> issued**:
>
> • **Adopt the full Infrastructure Program Plan** — every asset, prioritized by published
> criteria — completing the Vision 2050 roadmap;
> • **Publish lifecycle costs** — what each project will cost to operate and maintain, and
> how long it will last — before it is funded;
> • **Complete the Asset Management Program**, so new facilities never decay the way the old
> ones were allowed to;
> • **Create an Independent Citizens' Bond Oversight Committee** to certify, in public, that
> each bond sale follows the plan;
> • **Sequence the borrowing** — $100 million at a time over a decade — so debt matches
> delivery.
>
> Measure U costs about $22 per $100,000 of assessed value on average — roughly $161 a year
> for the median Berkeley home.
>
> The engineers and infrastructure professionals of the Mayor's Vision 2050 Task Force spent
> years demanding exactly this discipline. We support Measure U because these commitments
> deliver it. Waiting until 2028 adds tens of millions in construction inflation — and not
> one new guarantee.
>
> Berkeley doesn't need a perfect plan to start. It needs enforceable guarantees the work
> will follow one. Measure U now has them. **Vote Yes.**

**Honesty rails on this draft:** (1) the "committed, by resolution" sentence is only true if
the Council actually adopts the commitments resolution (§B) before ballots print — that
resolution is the price of the group's signatures; without it, soften to "will adopt" and
weigh whether to sign. (2) The $161 median figure is the advertised-average basis (median SFR
net AV $667k × $22.14/$100k); peak-rate basis is ~$233 — do not mix bases. (3) Adapt to quote
the actual filed argument against before submission.

## B. The actions that justify support — and the legal bodies that take them

The leverage: Measure U §6(F) assigns oversight to two named commissions **"or their
successors"** — the Council can therefore create every body below by ordinary ordinance or
resolution. **Nothing requires a charter amendment or a second election.** The group's
support should be exchanged for one omnibus **"Measure U Implementation & Accountability
Resolution"** adopted at a September 2026 Council meeting (before ballots drop), with the
ordinances following.

| # | Action (the condition) | Legal instrument | Body that acts (● = NEW) | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Adopt the Infrastructure Program Plan** (update the 2022 draft; Envision 60/40 criteria; full $2.1B need; phasing) — *before any issuance beyond the first tranche* | Council resolution adopting the Plan; incorporated by reference into the Debt Management Policy | ● **Capital Program Office** (new City Manager unit, created by budget ordinance) prepares; Council adopts; Oversight Committee (#3) reviews | Plan adopted ≤ June 2027; binds tranche 2 (2032) |
| 2 | **Lifecycle-cost discipline** — no bond appropriation without a published per-project statement of estimated annual O&M, design life, and the identified operating fund that will carry it (Larry Henry's Q1/Q2, made law) | **Ordinance** (new BMC chapter — a "fiscal lifecycle statement" requirement, analogous to CEQA/fiscal-impact statements) | City Manager / Capital Program Office produces; ● **Oversight Committee** certifies compliance | Ordinance ≤ Q1 2027; applies to every appropriation |
| 3 | **Independent Citizens' Bond Oversight Committee** — public members with financial/engineering qualifications; annual public report; **pre-issuance certification** that each sale conforms to the Plan; audit liaison to the City Auditor | **Ordinance** creating the committee as the **"successor"** oversight body under Measure U §6(F) — the measure's own text permits this substitution | ● **The Committee itself** (7–9 members, Council-appointed, staggered terms, no city employees) | Seated ≤ March 2027, before first sale closes |
| 4 | **Complete the Asset Management Program** (facilities, paving, sidewalks, sewers — the V2050 July 2027 target) and **establish a Facilities Maintenance Reserve Fund** with a funding policy (annual contribution set as a % of asset replacement value; anti-supplanting language hardened from the measure's hortatory whereas into law) | Resolution adopting the AMP; **ordinance** establishing the Reserve Fund (a new legal fund of the City) | ● **AMP Steering Committee** (V2050-recommended, City Manager–chartered) runs implementation; Finance Dept administers the fund | AMP ≤ July 2027; Fund in FY28 budget |
| 5 | **Strategic Financing Plan + tranche gating** — amend the City's Debt Management Policy so each $100M issuance requires: adopted Program Plan (#1), lifecycle statements (#2), Committee certification (#3), AMP milestones (#4) | Council **resolution amending the Debt Management Policy** (the document bond counsel and rating agencies read) | Finance Dept drafts; Council adopts; ● Oversight Committee certifies each gate | Policy amended ≤ first issuance (2027) |
| 6 | **Unified delivery organization + technology partnership** — consolidate cross-departmental capital delivery (V2050 Strategy 5); standing technical collaboration with UC Berkeley (Center for Smart Infrastructure) and LBNL for lifecycle-cost methods, pavement alternatives, green infrastructure | City Manager reorganization + budget ordinance; **MOU** with UC/LBNL ratified by Council | ● **Office of Infrastructure Program Delivery** (the Capital Program Office of #1, grown to delivery); ● **Technical Advisory Panel** under the MOU | Office FY28 budget; MOU ≤ 2027 |
| 7 | **Annual "State of the Infrastructure" report + public dashboard** — spending vs. plan, O&M obligations created, asset-condition trend; feeds the triennial City-Auditor audits already required by §6(G) | Council resolution (reporting requirement folded into #5's policy) | City Manager reports; ● Oversight Committee publishes its independent assessment alongside | First report FY28 |

**What is deliberately NOT on the list:** anything requiring voter approval, charter
amendment, or amendment of Measure U itself — every commitment is implementable by the
sitting Council, which is exactly what makes a September resolution a credible, checkable
promise rather than a campaign slogan.

**The trade, stated plainly:** the Task Force group provides signatures on the rebuttal (if
authorized by the primary-argument authors), public endorsement, and its professional
credibility; the Council provides the September resolution adopting #1–#7 with the dates
above. If the resolution fails, the group's support — and the rebuttal's central claim —
fails with it.
