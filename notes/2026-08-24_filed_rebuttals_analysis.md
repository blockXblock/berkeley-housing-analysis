## 1. Why these were never findable online

Not a search failure — a not-yet-published record:

1. **Timing.** Per the Clerk's instructions PDF: primaries due 12:00 p.m. Fri
   **Aug 14** (received stamps Aug 12–14 — the opposition filed early);
   rebuttals due 12:00 p.m. Fri **Aug 21** (stamps Aug 20–21). Arguments are

## 2. Gordon Wozniak — complete tracking across all 25 documents

**Primary round (Aug 12–14): Gordon appears NOWHERE.** He signed no primary
argument for any measure. The U and V primaries in favor were signed by Ishii,
Goldfarb (LWV), Caner, Weaver, and **Jenny Wong, Berkeley City Auditor** (see §4).

**Rebuttal round (Aug 20): Gordon appears twice, both on the YES side.**

1. **U pro-rebuttal — QUOTED (endorser):**
   > "Gordon Wozniak, Former Councilmember/Mayor's Vision 2050 Task Force Member:
   > 'Any suggestion that Vision 2050 recommendations are not the basis for
   > Measure U, is false. Vote YES on U…'"
2. **V pro-rebuttal — FULL SIGNATORY:** "Gordon Wozniak, Former
   Councilmember/Mayor's Vision 2050 Task Force."

**What this sequence means.** The Argument Against U (filed Aug 13) contains this
paragraph:
> "Berkeley residents devoted countless volunteer hours through 2025 to develop
> Vision 2050, a thoughtful comprehensive long-term strategy for the City's
> capital needs. Yet its recommendations have not been implemented or
> meaningfully incorporated into Measure U."

The Clerk transmits primaries to the opposing side immediately after the Aug 14
deadline. Gordon's entry is therefore a **direct response**: between Aug 14 and
Aug 20 the campaign recruited a Vision 2050 name to flatly deny the opposition's
V2050 claim, and he simultaneously joined the V rebuttal as a signer. Under the
Clerk's rules this is formal, on-file campaign participation — a quoted endorser
must sign a consent form, and a signatory signs the EC 9600 true-and-correct
declaration. **His quoted sentence is an absolute** ("any suggestion… is false")
that contradicts not only the opposition but the group's own published analysis,
which found Measure U implements part of Vision 2050 while omitting lifecycle
costing, prioritization, and major recommended programs.

**The V2050 tug-of-war (new, and central).** Both campaigns now claim Vision
2050's mantle in official ballot materials: the NO side praises V2050 and says U
ignores it; the YES side (via Gordon) says U is based on it and any suggestion
otherwise is false. The group — the people who actually wrote Vision 2050 — has
been conscripted by both sides in absolute terms. Evaluation No. 1's measured
middle ("implements part; omits X, Y, Z") is now the only accurate statement in
the debate, and it will be read against both campaigns' absolutes.

## 4. Measure U — the full four-document record

### Primary in favor (Ishii + Goldfarb + Caner + Weaver + Auditor Wong; Aug 14)
- Frames U as safety/climate: 911 dispatch, "replacing unsafe fire stations,"
  green infrastructure, **"constructing seawalls to prepare for sea-level rise"**
  — a notable stretch: the project list's shoreline item is a fraction of the
  $300M, and "seawalls" appears nowhere in the city's own list at that scale.
- "Robust, public project list, City Auditor and citizen oversight, regular
  public audits" — the ordinance's committee is advisory; and see the Wong
  problem below.
- "Makes Berkeley eligible for matching funds" — unquantified, no source.
- **⚠ Jenny Wong, sitting City Auditor, is a full SIGNER of the campaign
  argument** for the bond her office would audit. For Evaluation No. 1's Question
  4 (who oversees/audits?), the designated auditor has now signed the campaign's
  ballot argument — a structural independence problem worth stating neutrally.

### Primary against (Affordable Berkeley; Aug 13) — their strongest document
- "$550–600 million" total cost (still understates the TRS's ~$610M).
- "Council identified **35 potential** projects… did not prioritize or legally
  commit to any of them." (Our reference list carries 33 — the 35 likely counts
  alternates; minor, but worth reconciling.)
- Measure T1 (2016) cost overruns "about 15%"; "projects to double in cost"
  under procurement — plausible direction, unverified magnitudes.
- Specific mixed-purpose examples: $14M sidewalks (vs 2024 Measure FF's promise),
  $6.7M Old City Hall seismic "despite needing more than $100M," $25M King Pool
  (North/South equity point).
- **The Vision 2050 paragraph** (quoted in §2) — the opposition wields V2050
  as its central legitimacy weapon.
- Core demand mirrors the group's: "establish clear priorities, make binding
  commitments to bonafide capital projects, and present a credible plan for
  maintaining new facilities."

### Rebuttal to argument against (Ishii; Aug 20)
- "$1.5 BILLION infrastructure backlog" — understates the City's own $2.0–2.1B
  figure by ~$600M (an official campaign document contradicting the City's CIP).
- Wong endorsement "because it includes independent oversight" (advisory-only).
- The Wozniak quote (§2). Plan at tinyurl.com/2026BerkBond.

### Rebuttal to argument in favor (Affordable Berkeley; Aug 20)
- Repeats $550–600M; quotes 2024 FF voter-guide promises; "blank check for
  redundant purposes"; the lifecycle/priorities critique nearly verbatim from
  the group's published work.

**Net:** on Measure U, both sides misstate their own best numbers in opposite
directions, and the group holds the only correct interest-inclusive total
(~$610M from the City's own Tax Rate Statement) and the only quantified
lifecycle analysis.

## 5. The other measures — primaries + rebuttals

### V (½¢ sales tax)
- **Pro primary** (same five signers incl. Wong): federal-threat framing ("money
  the Federal government cannot take away"); names **Fire Station 4** closure
  and specific cuts. Deficit blamed on state/federal takeaways — our budget
  facts say the driver is structural (pensions, CalPERS $77.9M).
- **Anti primary** (Affordable Berkeley; adds Haselsteiner, Rader): sober and
  mostly accurate — 10.25%→10.75%, regional transport tax would stack to
  **11.25%**; **$29.5M structural deficit by FY2028** (matches our $29M);
  general-tax non-dedication ("available for any purpose"); "expenditures
  projected to grow faster than that new revenue." No salary theatrics (that
  appears only in their rebuttal).
- Neither side mentions **Sec. 7.34.150 securitization** (V revenue pledgeable
  to bonds) — still only in the group's analysis.

### W (charter amendment) — no opposition filed
Pro argument signed by Ishii, Bartlett, Blackaby, Tregub, Taplin. Uncontested.

### X (rent ordinance)
The primary-vs-rebuttal signer shuffle resolves yesterday's puzzle: the primary
AGAINST was filed by the **Bridge Association of REALTORS president + BPOA's
executive director** — so the pro-rebuttal's "for-profit landlord and realtor
lobbyists" line was aimed accurately at the primary's signers. The rebuttal
round then re-fronted the No side with the **city's own ADU Task Force
co-chairs** (Crandall, Sanderson) — with Gulbransen still the author. The pro
side's claim that BPOA "committed in writing not to oppose Measure X" sits in
direct contradiction with BPOA's ED authoring both No documents — one of those
is wrong, or the commitment was narrower than claimed. Substantive No claims:
Rent Board budget >$8M, ADU-chilling, fee-waiver burden-shift.

### Y (arts parcel tax)
- **Pro primary** (author Tom Parrish; signers incl. Chamber CEO, Freight ED,
  Sen. Arreguín, O'Keefe, **Wengraf**): 8,000 jobs, "$10 million in annual City
  revenue," pandemic-recovery framing. Grantee organizations are the campaign.
- **Anti primary** (Denton, solo): the adapt-or-bailout frame — praises the
  **Freight & Salvage** (66,500 tickets 2025) as the healthy model while its ED
  signs the pro side; claims Berkeley Rep raised $30M in the pandemic, then
  program revenue fell >$1M in 2025 after "niche political narratives"; the
  $630 gift-limit/no-aggregate-cap point. His rebuttal adds the sharper
  (libel-adjacent) free-tickets-then-$45k-grant allegation — quote only as a
  contested claim.
- The pro rebuttal's typed "$437.4 million in economic activity" is **hand-
  corrected to $137,400,000** on the received copy — a 3× overstatement caught
  at filing.

### Z (public bank)
- **Anti primary** (Affordable Berkeley) adds a strong NEW fact: the public-bank
  effort was originally a **regional** capitalization (~$40M shared by Oakland,
  Berkeley, Richmond, Alameda County; Berkeley's share ~$2.5M). That model never
  materialized — now Berkeley alone pays **$58.3M (100%+ of the original
  all-region cost)** for a bank that may never receive regulatory authorization,
  with a **mandatory escalator no Council can suspend** and no
  capitalization-achieved cutoff. Their most factually grounded filing.
- **Pro primary/rebuttal** (Tregub, Kelley, Holts, Leenson/La Peña, Singh):
  Bank of North Dakota ">$230M profit last year" (unverified, likely inflated);
  concedes regional lending; 2033 fallback restrictions accurate.

### AA (soda tax 1¢→2¢)
- **Pro primary** (author Anne Marice Ashe; Ecology Center ED, Rev. Michael
  Smith, AHA volunteer, Healthy Black Families, BUSD parent): 42.5%/42.5%/15%
  split, "not a parcel tax or sales tax," 5,000 signatures.
- **Anti primary** (Park): itemized grants — $108k health-app interns, $140k
  tap-water "systems change," $135k checkout-candy lobbying, $107k puppet shows;
  process-metrics critique ("exposure" and "intentions" as success measures);
  Richmond Costco leakage. (The Clerk's handwritten word counts run up the
  margin — 283 circled, under the 300 limit.)
- **Across all four AA documents, still no one mentions the ~$1.2M/yr General
  Fund LOSS** (AA suspends the existing 1¢ general tax). The group's analysis
  remains the only place this fact exists.

## 7. Implications for the group

1. **The V2050 tug-of-war makes Evaluation No. 1 the referee.** Both campaigns
   assert opposite absolutes about Vision 2050 in official ballot materials. The
   group's measured finding is the only accurate statement — and both sides will
   have reason to quote it selectively. The support-with-commitments frame must
   be unmistakable in the first paragraph.
2. **The Wozniak decision is now sharper and more urgent.** He is not merely
   sympathetic to the campaign — he is a consented, on-file campaign endorser
   (U) and signatory (V), recruited specifically to rebut a V2050-based
   critique, while co-authoring the group's "independent, campaign-unaffiliated"
   op-ed. Options remain John's call: disclosure line in Evaluation No. 1, a
   clarifying conversation, or his recusal from the evaluation's sign-off.
3. **The Auditor's dual role belongs in Question 4.** Jenny Wong signed the
   campaign's primary arguments for U and V; the measure's oversight design
   leans on "City Auditor and citizen oversight." Evaluation No. 1 can state
   this neutrally: the designated audit function has publicly campaigned for
   the measure it would audit.
4. **The group holds unique, debate-absent facts:** the true ~$610M
   interest-inclusive cost, the V securitization clause, the AA general-fund
   loss, and the quantified lifecycle/NRC analysis. Nobody else in 25 filed
   documents has any of them.
5. **Republication care:** the Y gift/vote allegation (rebuttal), the X
   "bad-faith lobbyists" line, and the Berkeley Rep salary/programming claims
   are litigation-adjacent — attribute strictly as contested campaign claims.
6. **Reconcile the 33-vs-35 project count** against the Council referral item
   before the evidence site cites either number as "the list."
