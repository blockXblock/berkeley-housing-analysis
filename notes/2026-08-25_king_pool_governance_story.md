# Who Did What: the King Pool Re-Scope and the Bond List's Governance Trail

**Date:** 2026-08-25 · **For:** Vision 2050 working group
**Primary sources (all preserved in `data/raw/ballot_args_2026/`):** Dec 2 2025
Council staff report; Feb 19 2026 T&I Commission packet (the $25M-King list
version); Mar 17 2026 Council survey-results slides; PRW Commission Apr 8 2026
packet (Bond Measure Subcommittee Final Report) and May 13 packet (Apr 8
minutes); the filed-program list (berkeleyca.gov + July council packet).

## The chain of custody

1. **Jun 17 / Sep 30, 2025 — Council referrals** to the City Manager: assess
   2026 measures for fire facilities, then recreation/community facilities.
2. **Oct 8, 2025 — PRW Commission forms its Bond Measure Subcommittee**
   (Leigh Stewart, Mary Muszynski, Ben Nash).
3. **Dec 2, 2025 — Council work session** ("the 12-2-25 Action"): staff present
   needs; Council gives *input and direction* on which projects go into a
   community survey. No list or amounts adopted.
4. **Jan–Feb 2026 — staff publish the priced list** ("35 Potential Bond Measure
   Projects per 12-2-25 City Council Action"): **King Pool $25M full
   replacement** (new 25-meter pool replacing the 25-yard + dive pools),
   Albrier $30M, **Old City Hall seismic $6.72M**, Parks subtotal $107.6M.
   Circulated to 6 commissions, 4 focus groups, 4 joint district community
   meetings; first survey (Lake Research, ~500 voters) runs on it.
5. **Mar 3, 2026** — PRW Director briefs the subcommittee on polling
   (pre-public): **$300M polled at 72%** — stronger than $200M; waterfront
   polls poorly, climate polls well.
6. **Mar 11, 2026 — the PRW Commission unanimously adopts the subcommittee's
   Final Report** and votes to send it to Council as an Information Item.
7. **Mar 17, 2026 — Council sees survey results**, directs the City
   Manager/City Attorney to draft $300M bond language "aligned with survey
   results" + authorizes a second survey. Slides disclose the portfolio rule:
   **a $100M per-category cap** — which the Feb Parks list ($107.6M) violated.
8. **Apr 14, 2026 — the Commission's report reaches Council** (Information
   Calendar; Commissioner Stewart authorized Apr 8 to speak to it in person).
9. **Apr–Jul 2026 — staff re-scope, with no public decision point:** King
   $25M replacement → **$5M decking/piping repair**; Old City Hall seismic
   $6.72M → **$3.6M "partial"**; Albrier $30M → $36.85M; Parks $107.6M →
   $90.7M. The same-titled PDF is replaced in place on berkeleyca.gov; the
   "(25 Meter)" title survives on a line that no longer builds a 25-meter pool
   (the existing King pool is 25 *yards*).
10. **Jul 28, 2026 — Council adopts the referral package** (Resolution
    72,338-N.S. + companions) with the revised exhibit. **Jun 10 / Jul 8 PRW
    agendas contain no bond item** — the Commission never saw or voted on the
    revised list.

## The Commission's report (what Council was told in April)

The report is enthusiastically pro-bond ("we want it to pass") and reviews all
20 PRW projects at the Feb prices. Its five recommendations:

- **A. "The pool strategy needs more work."** The city **owns no pool site**:
  King and West Campus both sit on **BUSD land**; Willard was lost when BUSD
  reclaimed the site in 2010; West Campus has **4 years left on its lease**;
  King has 24 years — but **the City Attorney requires a minimum 25-year lease
  for bond-funded construction**, so King's lease must be renegotiated before
  bond money could build there. Recommended seriously considering a pool at
  **San Pablo Park** (city-owned land, adjacent to the underserved SE
  quadrant), and securing site agreements "before going to voters." BUSD
  superintendent conversations were "encouraging, but informal."
- **B. No formal scoring existed** — the PRW Director confirmed it; criteria
  were never documented as applied.
- **C. Climate framing** — waterfront projects poll poorly but ARE climate
  projects; make it visible.
- **D. Civic Center allocation "difficult to justify"** — Old City Hall/Vets
  seismic on closed buildings met "consistently tepid to negative" community
  reaction; seismic-only spending "is the first installment on a much larger
  obligation" (full rehab "hundreds of millions"); recommend justify or
  reallocate.
- **E. Southeast Berkeley: zero PRW projects** in the quadrant with the fewest
  park facilities; "southeast" appears nowhere in the 50-page staff report.

## What happened to the recommendations

The **risk half of recommendation A was acted on silently**: cutting King to a
$5M repair sidesteps the 25-year-lease bar on bond *construction* and pulls
Parks under the $100M cap. But the **affirmative half was ignored**: no San
Pablo pool, no lease-security commitment, no SE-quadrant project, no scoring
documentation, and the Civic Center items stayed (halved, unannounced,
unjustified). No staff report, council item, or public communication ever
announced or explained the changes — they are visible only by diffing the
February and July versions of a document that kept the same title.

## Why this matters for the group's work

- **The opposition's "wrong" numbers ($25M King, $6.7M Old City Hall, 35
  projects) are all correct citations of the February outreach version.** The
  goalposts moved after the community saw the list.
- **The "list can change at any time" critique is not a prediction — it is
  documented history**, twice over, before a single vote is cast.
- **The pool-strategy facts (BUSD land, 25-year-lease bar, Willard precedent,
  West Campus 4-year lease) are the Commission's own findings** — citable
  against both campaigns, and they make the filed $5M King line look less like
  a project and more like a placeholder.
- The September implementation resolution's demands (prioritization criteria
  documented, lifecycle costing, binding list discipline) are the same asks
  the City's own Parks Commission made in April — and were not answered.
