# Notes on Your Wellstone Draft — Corrections and Stronger Citations

**From the Vision 2050 working group · August 25, 2026**

Jim — your draft is substantively right about what Measure U lacks. But several
numbers need correction before you present, and there is stronger, citable
ammunition you're not yet using. Everything below is verified against the City's
own filed documents.

## King Pool: get the two versions right — it's a stronger point than either number

There are **two versions of the City's own project list**, both titled
"Potential Bond Measure Projects (per 12-2-25 City Council Action)," and they
disagree about King Pool by a factor of five:

- **The version presented at the February 2026 community meetings and
  commissions** (in the Feb 19, 2026 Transportation & Infrastructure Commission
  agenda packet): "King Pool (25 Meter) & Locker Room — **$25,000,000** — Full
  Replacement/Modernization… **builds new 25 Meter pool to replace existing 25
  yard and dive pools.**" Parks subtotal: $107.6M.
- **The filed bond program** (the PDF now served on berkeleyca.gov and the July
  2026 council packet — the list the $300M program math is built on): the same
  line title, now **$5,000,000** — "replaces concrete decking and piping."
  Parks subtotal: $90.7M.

**Between the public outreach and the filing, the flagship aquatics project was
cut roughly 80% — from a new pool to a repair — with the "(25 Meter)" name left
in place** (the existing King pool is 25 *yards*). Frances Albrier moved the
other direction, $30.0M → $36.85M.

So do not say "$25 million for King Pool" as the filed opposition argument does
— the campaign will "correct" you with the $5M line and use it to dismiss the
rest. Say the stronger, fully documented thing instead: *"The $25 million
replacement pool the public was shown in February became a $5 million repair in
the filed list. The list can change at any time — because it already has."*
That is your "list could change" argument proven with the City's own
before-and-after. And **Willard Pool appears in neither version** — the only
Willard items anywhere in the City's lists are for the Willard *Clubhouse*, a
different facility.

## Corrections to other figures

- **Old City Hall: $3.6 million, not $3.3M** — and the City's own list states
  the full seismic cost is **$13,942,000**, so the bond funds roughly a quarter
  of even the seismic work and none of the restoration. Your "won't even cover
  the needed seismic repairs" is correct, citable from the City's document. The
  ">$100 million restoration" figure comes from Affordable Berkeley's argument
  and is unverified — attribute it or drop it. (Same pattern next door: the
  Veterans Memorial Building gets $1.8M "partial" seismic that does not return
  the building to use.)
- **Sidewalks: $14.6 million**, specifically the City's half of the 50/50
  program. On Measure FF, our records show **~$15M/yr over 14 years**, not $17M
  — pull the exact figure from the FF measure text. The "collected but not
  spent" claim needs a citation (the FF annual report or a budget document);
  without one it's the easiest line for the campaign to deny.
- **"About 30 years to spend" is wrong — the truth is better for you.** The
  official structure is three $100M tranches, roughly 2027/2032/2037, with
  final repayment in **FY2066–67**. Issued over about a decade; **not paid off
  for forty years**. Taxpayers in 2066 will still be paying for HVAC installed
  in the late 2020s.

## Ammunition you don't have yet (all citable)

1. **Your own quote is even weaker than you say.** The "non-exhaustive list…
   should be guided" language is a **WHEREAS recital** in Resolution
   72,338-N.S. — preamble, not operative text. Recitals bind nothing, and even
   the recital only says "should." The operative sections impose no project
   obligations at all.
2. **The list doesn't even add up to $300M.** The 36 listed items total
   **$272.5M** — roughly $27.5M of the authorization has no named project even
   on the non-binding list.
3. **Short-life assets on 40-year debt, quantified:** **$47.7M** of the list
   (≈18%) is assets with under-25-year lives, and **$68.6M** sits on assets
   expected to need replacement *again before the bonds are repaid* — the
   poster child is 2180 Milvia's $11.7M HVAC/electrical/plumbing package
   (20–25-year life, 40-year financing). This turns your carpets-and-HVAC
   sentence into a number.
4. **Total cost, from the City's own Tax Rate Statement: $610 million** in
   total debt service ($22.14 per $100k of assessed value on average, peaking
   at $35 from about 2040; implied borrowing rate ~5.4%). The City's own
   official figure is worse than the opposition's "$550–600M."
5. **Maintenance: nothing, against the City's own policy.** The measure
   contains no O&M estimate or funding. Our analysis puts the new recurring
   cost of the built projects at **$1.6–3.3M per year**, landing on a General
   Fund already running a ~$30M structural deficit — directly contrary to the
   City's adopted asset management policy you cite.
6. **Oversight is advisory-only.** The committee reviews and reports; it
   selects nothing and can stop nothing.
7. **Context for "no priorities": the City's own capital-needs figure is
   $2.0–2.1 billion.** The bond covers about one-seventh of the identified
   need — which is exactly why an unprioritized list is a real problem.

## The September test

Attached is a draft resolution the Council could adopt in September: binding
prioritization before issuance, lifecycle costing for every project, debt term
matched to asset life, maintenance funded before construction, oversight with
authority. The measure itself can't be amended — but these commitments can be
made binding on the proceeds before a single vote is cast. If you end your
presentation with "here is what Council could adopt in September; if they will
not even do this, vote No," your opposition becomes a falsifiable test instead
of a prediction — and the burden shifts to the Council, where it belongs.
