# DRAFT — Amended version of the September 29 referral (for discussion)

**ACTION CALENDAR**
September 29, 2026

**TO:** Honorable Mayor and Members of the City Council
**FROM:** Mayor Adena Ishii (Author)
**SUBJECT:** Referral to the City Manager to Develop a Comprehensive
Cross-Departmental Program Plan and Strategic Asset Management Plan to Fully
Implement the Realize Vision 2050 Plan; and Adoption of a Resolution
Establishing Interim Requirements Governing the Expenditure of Measure U
Bond Proceeds

## RECOMMENDATION

1. Refer to the City Manager the development of a comprehensive
   cross-departmental **Program Plan** and **Strategic Asset Management
   Plan** to fully implement the Realize Vision 2050 Plan, addressing the
   City's long-term infrastructure needs and outlining necessary maintenance
   and schedule; **with a report back to Council within 120 days, and in any
   event before the issuance of the first series of bonds under Measure U**,
   should the voters approve it.

2. Adopt a Resolution establishing the following requirements, which shall
   govern all expenditures of Measure U bond proceeds:

   a. **Prioritization before spending.** No bond proceeds shall be
      expended on a project until the Program Plan's published
      prioritization criteria have been applied to that project and the
      result reported to Council.

   b. **Lifecycle costing.** Before any project receives a construction
      appropriation of bond proceeds, the City Manager shall publish a
      lifecycle cost analysis identifying the project's expected useful
      life, recurring operation and maintenance cost, and the funding
      source for that maintenance, consistent with the City's Asset
      Management Policy (adopted May 10, 2022).

   c. **Independent audit.** The City Auditor shall audit the expenditure
      of each bond series, and the results shall be presented to Council
      and posted publicly.

   d. **Public dashboard, required.** The City Manager shall establish and
      maintain a public dashboard tracking every bond-funded project's
      budget, expenditures, schedule, and change orders, updated at least
      quarterly. The dashboard shall include a **published change log**: any
      change to a project's scope or estimated cost shall be recorded with
      the date and reason for the change.

## CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS

The City of Berkeley has identified approximately **$1.87 billion** in
capital and maintenance needs (FY2027 estimate presented to Council on
March 17, 2026), against average available annual funding of roughly $39
million. The City is taking significant steps to address this need, but to
do so effectively it must understand the full landscape of the need and
apply published criteria for setting priorities.

To accomplish this, the City needs a **Program Plan** — a high-level
strategic document outlining the City's infrastructure projects citywide,
with cost analysis and a prioritization schema informed by urgency and the
Realize Vision 2050 plan — and a **Strategic Asset Management Plan** — a
roadmap for managing the City's physical assets over their full life
cycles, so that every capital decision accounts for the maintenance
obligation it creates.

These plans must not remain aspirational. The requirements in
Recommendation 2 bind the *process* by which bond funds are spent — not the
projects themselves. Circumstances will change over a decade of bond
issuance: costs move, grants appear, site conditions and agreements evolve,
and the City Manager must retain the flexibility to respond. What these
requirements guarantee is that when priorities change, the change is made
against published criteria, with its lifecycle consequences stated, and
recorded publicly — flexibility with a paper trail. This directly addresses
the most persistent public criticism of Measure U: that nothing in the
measure binds the use of the funds.

To promote transparency, the public dashboard required in Recommendation
2(d) may be modeled on the Transportation Studio: Capital Improvement
Program (CIP) Hub created by UC Berkeley students, tracking current project
schedules and budgets and integrating future capital planning.

## BACKGROUND

The City of Berkeley maintains over 300 miles of sidewalks, 54 parks, 95
buildings, as well as municipal drains, streets and much more. In 2020, the
City put forth the Vision 2050 plan, "a long-term infrastructure plan to
create a city that is resilient and sustainable for future generations."
Following the November 16, 2021 Vision 2050 Progress Report, on May 10,
2022 the City Council approved the Vision 2050 Strategic Asset Management
Plan and Asset Management Policy. In 2025, Mayor Adena Ishii reconvened the
Vision 2050 Task Force to update the plan; the updated plan, **Realize
Vision 2050**, reiterates the need for both an Infrastructure Program Plan
and an Asset Management Plan.

The 2022 adoption demonstrates that adopting a plan is not the same as
implementing one. This item therefore pairs the planning referral with
operative requirements and a deadline, so that the plans arrive before the
money moves.

## ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY

There are no environmental impacts generated by this referral. The
lifecycle costing required in Recommendation 2(b) will surface the energy
and electrification characteristics of each project, supporting the City's
climate goals.

## FISCAL IMPACTS

Development of the Program Plan and Asset Management Plan requires staff
time across departments. The audit requirement falls within the City
Auditor's existing authority; audit costs of each bond series are an
eligible cost of bond administration. The dashboard can be built on
existing City platforms; modest one-time configuration costs are
anticipated.
