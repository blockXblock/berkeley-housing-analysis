# Save Berkeley Shops: every event from the City's August 2025 corridor presentation to today

A chronology assembled for the history-of-the-website piece. **Read the three corrections
first** — two of them change the shape of the story.

---

## Three corrections to the premise

**1. "Rahini and associates" is two different things, and you'll want both.**

- **Raimi + Associates** is the planning consultancy the City hired to run the Corridors
  Zoning Update (CZU) — the contractor that authored the Existing Conditions Report
  (5 Mar 2025) and the Alternatives Report, and ran the corridor workshops. Team: Raimi +
  Associates (lead), Plan to Place, Yes Duffy Architects, Strategic Economics, Rincon,
  Kittelson. The campaign's own March 2026 appeal puts the contract at **$600,000**.
- **Rohini Kumar** is a person on the other side of the table: North Shattuck resident
  representative and **Treasurer of Save Berkeley Shops**.

Near-homophones, opposite roles. Worth a footnote in the piece rather than a silent fix.

**2. August 2025 was not the *initial* presentation — it was the first time Elmwood saw
its own street drawn.** The City's published CZU timeline has **Community Workshop #1 on
1 May 2025** (citywide, generic). The August event is **Community Workshop #2, 20 August
2025**, and what made it different is that it was **corridor-specific**: separate
presentations and design boards for North Shattuck, College Avenue, and Solano Avenue.
That distinction is the actual ignition point. People did not mobilise when the City
proposed upzoning. They mobilised when the consultant showed them a massing diagram of
*their* block.

**3. The organizing is younger than the outrage, and the gap is ~7 weeks.** The Aug 20
workshop produced no visible email traffic in the record. The first organized Elmwood
meeting in the mail is **12–13 October 2025**. The name "Save Berkeley Shops" and a
website arrive **16 October 2025**. Whatever happened in those seven weeks happened in
person, in shops, and is not in any file I can read — see *Gaps*.

## Phase III — The hearing (6 Nov 2025)

**City Council Worksession #1.** Six hours, on Granicus (clip 5532).

Two things in this record are worth the piece's whole middle section:

- **33 named public speakers**, each with an individual YouTube link in John's table —
  shopkeepers (Berkeley Organic Market, Bill's Trading Post, Nabolom Bakery, Fern's
  Garden, In The Wood, Cheese Board Collective), realtors, a Solano social worker, an
  urban planner, Patrick Kennedy (the developer), and **Soni Trauss and a YIMBY organizer
  speaking the other way**. It is not a one-sided record, and the campaign archived both
  sides.
- **124 written communications** tabulated from the supplemental packet — and **32 of
  them carry the identical subject line "Elmwood Families in Support of Corridor
  Upzoning."** A second bloc arrives via East Bay YIMBY. So the mobilisation ran in both
  directions and both sides used list infrastructure. Any honest history has to say that;
  the interesting question is not which side organized but that *both* discovered the same
  tooling within weeks.

Council "reviewed draft zoning alternatives and provided direction on next steps" (City
minutes). The campaign's reading, from the March appeal: when residents pushed back,
Council responded by **raising** the height ceiling — the appeal claims a move toward 14
storeys and "a 1,400% height increase in many cases." *Present that as the campaign's
characterisation, not as fact; it is the one load-bearing claim I could not verify against
the City record.*

## What the record actually shows, for the political question

Five findings that are in the evidence rather than in anybody's talking points:

1. **The trigger was specificity, not policy.** Citywide Workshop #1 (1 May 2025)
   produced nothing. Corridor-specific Workshop #2 (20 Aug 2025) produced a movement.
   People mobilise against a drawing of their own block.

2. **The seven-week silence is the most interesting artefact in the file.** Aug 20 to
   Oct 11: no email, no list, no name. Then a fully-formed meeting, a website within
   four days, and a named organization within five. Something happened face-to-face in
   the Elmwood in September 2025 that left no digital trace. **That is the reporting
   hole**, and it is also the answer to the generational question — the thing that scaled
   was a shop-counter conversation, and what the tooling did was *catch* it.

3. **Both sides discovered the same infrastructure at the same time.** 32 identical
   pro-upzoning subject lines and an East Bay YIMBY write-in call sit in the same Nov 6
   packet as the shopkeepers' letters. The story is not NIMBY vs YIMBY organizing; it is
   that a municipal comment packet stopped being a record of individuals within one
   election cycle.

4. **The campaign's repeated substantive complaint is procedural, and it is checkable.**
   "The consultants spoke to developers but not to any of the businesses or their mom-and-
   pop landlords"; "the City bypassed the planning process it uses everywhere else
   [Adeline, San Pablo]." The "SECOND REQUEST" for a shop-owners meeting, eight months
   in, is the single most damaging document in the file — and the newly-obtained Raimi
   geodatabase is now the way to test it on the merits.

5. **It ends in the middle, which is the honest place to end.** No ordinance adopted. A
   referendum threatened but not filed. A council seat contested. A position that has
   moved from *stop this* to *housing AND shops* to *we want to help implement middle
   housing*. Whatever the piece argues, it cannot argue that the story is over.

   petition's origin (who wrote it, when), the Organic Market and Bill's Trading Post,
   and Carla Woodworth / Claudia Hunka / David Salk directly. This needs interviews.
3. **The "1,400% / 14 storeys" claim.** Verify against the Council's actual 6 Nov
   direction before repeating it in any voice.
4. **Alternatives Report date** — 15 or 18 Aug 2025. Check the PDF.
5. **The Granicus 6-hour video** (clip 5532) is the only complete record of the hearing;
   John's 33 speaker clips are the indexed version of it. Worth saying plainly in the
   piece that the campaign built a better index of a public hearing than the city did.

   minutes for that window.
7. **The May 2026 Planning Commission outcome and anything after 6 May** is not in the
   City's published timeline — confirm whether the CZU has moved since.
