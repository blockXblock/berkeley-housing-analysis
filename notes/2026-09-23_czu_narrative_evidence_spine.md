# The Corridors Zoning Update: an evidence spine

Everything below is from the City's own published documents (31 fetched, `data/raw/czu/`,
sha256 manifest), the Raimi geodatabase (CPRA #26-2367), the Rent Board registry
(CPRA #26-2375), Berkeley business licences and the Alameda assessor. Each claim is
traceable. **The caveats are part of the document, not an appendix** — several of my own
first answers in this line of work were wrong, and the corrections are recorded so nobody
repeats them.

---

## 1. Where the corridors came from: nobody chose them recently

**Housing Element Program 27**, adopted by Council and certified by HCD in 2023:

> "…increase allowed densities and/or development capacity with the goal of achieving
> consistency among all transit and commercial corridors, especially between formerly
> red-lined areas and higher-resource areas of **Solano Avenue, north Shattuck Avenue, and
> College Avenue**."
> **Timeline:** "By December 2026… increase new housing opportunities by **at least 2000
> units** on commercial and transit corridors." **Lead:** Planning.

So College Avenue's inclusion predates the consultant, the workshops and the Commission.
**No document attributes the corridor selection to any individual councilmember.** Claims of
individual credit are not supported by the written record — which is not the same as
disproved, but it is where the evidence sits.

The **parcel-by-parcel ranking methodology** is not explained in any deliverable. It would be
specified in the consultant's scope of work. → **CPRA target: the scope of work and any
written direction regarding site-level redevelopment-potential analysis.**

**The program misses its own target.** Program 27 promised ≥2,000 units. The adopted
approach projects **1,518**.

## 2. The size of the thing: 1.5 acres

From the consultant's own display boards at the **20 August 2025** corridor workshop — the
meeting that triggered the campaign:

| corridor | total potential (A+B+C) | very low / none | A. High | B. Modest | C. Special |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **College Ave** | **1.5 ac — 22%** | 5.4 ac — 66% | 1.0 ac — 16% | 0.5 ac — 7% | **0 ac** |
| North Shattuck | 6.7 ac — 37% | 11.4 ac — 63% | 3.4 ac — 19% | 1.3 ac — 7% | 2.0 ac — 11% |
| Solano Ave | 6.4 ac — 63% | 3.7 ac — 37% | 0.9 ac — 10% | 4.1 ac — 40% | 1.3 ac — 13% |

**College has the least redevelopment potential of the three, and the consultant said so in
public in August 2025.**

The May 2026 staff report agrees, from the other direction — projected units over existing
conditions under the staff-recommended option:

| | College | Solano | North Shattuck | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| units | **131** | 442 | 963 | **1,518** |
| on-site affordable (10%) | **13** | 44 | 97 | 152 |

Affordable units may be satisfied by in-lieu fee rather than built.

Against a 6th-cycle RHNA obligation of **8,934** and identified citywide capacity of
**19,098** (15,153 Housing Element + 1,200 BART + 2,745 other rezonings), **College Avenue is
1.5% of the obligation.** All three corridors together are 17%.

**Neither campaign has been using these numbers.** Save Berkeley Shops' March 2026 appeal
says "8–12 story residential towers", later "14 stories" and "a 1,400% height increase".
WECAN's College page names three sites and a five-storey limit — closer to the record.

## 3. What actually happened on 6 May 2026

The FINAL minutes are **not on the CZU page** and are a **scanned image with no text layer**
(4 characters extract). OCR'd; text at `data/raw/czu/2026-05-06_pc4__FINAL_minutes.ocr.txt`.

**Motion (Twu/Owens) — PASSED 6–2–0–1.** Ayes: Hauser, Vincent, Twu, Wang, Owens, Merker.
Noes: Oatfield, Kahn. Absent: Marthinsen.

It adopted the **Alternative 1B map**, added a floor (College 5 storeys, Solano 6, North
Shattuck 7), allowed Administrative Use Permit approval, removed lot-coverage and massing
standards, and committed to re-examine in the 7th Cycle. An Oatfield/Kahn substitute to adopt
**staff's** recommendation with small-business support **failed 2–6**. 74 public comments.

**Staff recommended Option 1A and were overruled.** The report is explicit — *"Staff recommend
the Map-Based Sites approach (Option 1A)"* — and justifies it on exactly the ground at issue:
*"while also minimizing business disruption or displacement pressure on existing small
businesses."* The staff report is authored **FROM Uttara Ramakrishnan, Associate Planner**;
Justin Horner, Principal Planner, is contact on the EIR.

**Attribution matters here.** Staff drafted both options and advised against the one adopted.
The body that chose the criteria-based path over the professionally graded map was the
Planning Commission, 6–2.

## 4. The mechanism failure

- **Option 1A (rejected):** "a defined set of specific parcels" — a named map informed by the
  consultant's site-by-site grading.
- **Option 1B (adopted):** objective criteria — ≥7,000 sf lot, ≥80 ft depth, corner location,
  **absence of existing residential units**, not a historic resource.

The consultant's grade **`4A` = "D. Very Low / No redevelopment potential"**, defined on the
workshop boards as *"Sites with Thriving Businesses, Construction Staging Challenges, or
Potential Historic Significance."*

**Parcel 052-1410-016-01** (3160–3190 College + 2625–2675 Alcatraz) **passes all five Option 1B
criteria** — 14,411 sf, ~116 ft deep, corner, zero rent-board units, not historic — **and is
graded `4A`.** On it: The Crow Law Firm, Law Office of Elva Harding, Kodama Diseño, UPS Store
#1069, Siamese Dream, Heritage Thai Spa, About Face and Body, Everything Set.

**The objective test is blind to the one thing category D exists to see.** Lot size, depth,
corner, residential units and historic status cannot detect a thriving business district.

A second-order effect, unremarked anywhere: because the test excludes parcels with residential
units, it **preferentially selects fully-commercial corners** — the ones without apartments
above the shops. And because business health is an input to the consultant's grade but not to
the criteria, **a struggling business makes its own parcel score better.**

## 5. AB 2011 is never mentioned

| term | occurrences across all 31 documents |
|---|---|
| **AB 2011** | **0** |
| **"ministerial"** | **0** |
| SB 6 | 2 |
| "by right" | 2 |
| density bonus | 244 |

Two years of commercial-corridor rezoning analysis — Existing Conditions, Alternatives, four
Planning Commission reports, a Council worksession, an EIR addendum — without one reference to
the state law that permits by-right multifamily housing on commercial corridors.

**But check the widths before drawing the conclusion.** From the Existing Conditions report:

| corridor | right-of-way | parcel depth |
|---|---|---|
| Shattuck | ~95 ft | 120 ft |
| Solano | ~90 ft | 115–120 ft |
| **College** | **~60 ft** | **80–120 ft** |

AB 2011 gates eligibility on right-of-way width. College at 60 ft may fall **below** the
statutory threshold — which would be an argument *for* rezoning it, not against. **Read the
thresholds off the statute before using this.**

The defensible finding is procedural: **the City never analyzed AB 2011**, so it never
established whether it was rezoning the corridors where rezoning adds most, or the ones where
state law already does the work.

## 6. Economic health of the commercial districts — what is and isn't measurable

Berkeley business licences: **12,971 records, every one carrying employee count, NAICS and
APN.** Joined to corridor parcels by canonical APN:

| corridor | businesses | employees | acres | biz/acre | emp/acre | emp/business |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| College Ave | 237 | 624 | 26.6 | 8.9 | 24 | 2.6 |
| North Shattuck | 204 | 1,000 | 16.8 | 12.1 | 60 | 4.9 |
| Solano Ave | 196 | 658 | 9.7 | 20.2 | 68 | 3.4 |

College's top sectors by employment: **food service 151, education 97, personal services 90,
arts/recreation 70, retail 62**.

⚠ **Confound:** College's parcel set includes ~147 residential parcels in the R-2A envelope,
which the other two corridors don't have. Its per-acre figures are therefore diluted and are
**not** comparable as published. Restrict to commercially zoned parcels before quoting.
⚠ Employee counts are self-reported and 4,611 of 12,971 licences report zero. **A floor.**
⚠ 94 real-estate licences on College with 50 employees between them are mostly individual
agents registered at an address, not storefronts.

**Not measurable from anything we hold:**
- **Revenue per block.** Not in the licence data. Berkeley's tax is gross-receipts-based but
  only the rate *category* (`tax_code`: C, L, P, R, B…) is recorded, not receipts.
- **Sales tax per block.** Parcel-level sales-tax data is confidential under state law; CDTFA
  publishes aggregates only. A city-level series is obtainable; a block-level one is not.
- **DoorDash delivery miles.** No dataset we can reach supports this. Any "delivery miles
  today vs a few years ago" figure would be a model resting on assumed order volumes, assumed
  trip chaining and assumed origins — **an estimate dressed as a measurement.** If it is
  wanted, it must be presented as a scenario with its assumptions on the face of it.

## 7. Food access: Berkeley is well served, and the Elmwood best of all

Distance from each neighborhood centroid to the nearest full-line grocery (NAICS 4451,
convenience and liquor excluded), 24 official neighborhoods:

| | km to full-line |
|---|---|
| **median, all neighborhoods** | **0.52 km** |
| neighborhoods > 1 km | **3 of 24** |
| **Elmwood District** | **0.59 km** (Star Grocery) — **0.01 km to any food retail** |
| Berkeley Hills | 1.36 km — the worst served |
| Terrace View / Cragmont | 1.16 / 1.02 km |

**The three worst-served neighborhoods are all in the hills** — which is where **24.8% of the
city's opportunity sites** sit, against the Elmwood's 2.7%.

⚠ Two classifier bugs to fix first: "VIV & INGRID ACCESSORIES" is miscoded NAICS 4451, and
"BOBS LIQUOR & DELI" evaded the liquor filter because its description misspells *"LIQOUR"*.
⚠ Straight-line distance, not walking network distance. Overstates access in the hills, where
the street network is least direct — so the hills' disadvantage is **understated** here.

**Next, and worth doing properly:** walking-network distance via OSM, and the mobility-based
approach — travel-behavior analysis of who actually goes where, in the tradition of
**Marta González's** urban-mobility work at UC Berkeley (mobile-phone-derived travel demand,
human-mobility modeling). That is the honest way to ask "how well does this commercial
district serve its residents", and it is a different question from "how many square feet of
shopfront does it have."

## 8. The Elmwood and AFFH — the strongest-sounding, legally weakest section

The density evidence is solid and baseline-gated (`JN-M_corridor_density`): **Elmwood District
12.8 du/acre against 6.9 for non-student Berkeley — 1.85×**; the College corridor about
15 du/ac; 69% of Elmwood blocks denser than the non-student median.

**Handle with care. AFFH is not a density test.** The obligation concerns access for protected
classes and income groups in high-resource areas. A century of dense student rentals is a real
and underappreciated fact about the Elmwood and it complicates the "exclusionary" framing — it
does not answer the statutory obligation. **A section resting on density alone is the first
paragraph a hostile reader takes apart.**

The version that holds: the Elmwood's housing has been added **residentially** — flats,
conversions, ADUs, student rentals — invisibly to a commercial-corridor framing, while
Program 27's 2,000-unit target is pursued through a 1.5-acre commercial strip yielding 131
units, and the residential fabric carries far more capacity under Middle Housing.

## 9. Corrections to my own work, recorded so they are not repeated

1. **Assessor `Units` counts commercial bays.** 2901 College reads "5 units" and is a toy shop
   plus retail frontage. Using it as a residential test wrongly eliminated parcels.
2. **APN joins silently return nothing.** Rent Board stores `052156801101`; the geodatabase
   stores `52-1567-24-1`. Strip-non-alphanumeric never matches — it returned zero units for all
   198 College parcels. **Always `housing_rules.to_canonical_apn`.**
3. **Many-to-many joins explode.** A naive licence↔parcel merge produced 112,488 businesses on
   College against 13,004 citywide. Dedupe the parcel side first and assert the row count.
4. **My depth proxy under-measures.** A minimum-rotated-rectangle short side reads frontage,
   not depth: it gave 40–70 ft where the consultant documents College parcels at **80–120 ft**.
   **Every depth figure I produced is unreliable.** The 80-foot criterion needs surveyed
   dimensions from the County parcel maps.
5. **Address-based corner detection under-counts.** It missed 2887 College and 2701 Webster,
   both visibly corners. Use street-centreline intersections instead.
