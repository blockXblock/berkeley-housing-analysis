# Sanborn fire-insurance maps — the Elmwood in 1911 and 1950

**What this is.** Two primary-source drawings of the same Berkeley blocks, 39 years apart,
at building-by-building resolution, with the **number of storeys written inside every
footprint**. This is the historical block record we went looking for in
[2026-09-24_historical_block_density_sources.md](2026-09-24_historical_block_density_sources.md),
and it is better than expected: Sanborn records storeys, use, and construction material,
which is exactly the input a historical floor-area (FAR) series needs and which no modern
assessor extract can supply for pre-war Berkeley.

Reproduce with `scripts/fetch_sanborn_elmwood.py` (sheets, gitignored) then
`scripts/gen_sanborn_elmwood_figure.py` (the comparison figure, committed at
`docs/images/elmwood_sanborn_1911_1950.jpg`).

## What is actually available free, and what is not

Verified against the LoC item API on 2026-09-24, not assumed:

| Berkeley vol. 2 edition | LoC volume id | Images exposed? |
|---|---|---|
| 1911 | `g00419191102` | **yes** — 2 index sheets + numbered sheets |
| 1950 | `g00419195002` | **yes** — 3 index sheets + numbered sheets |
| 1917, 1929 | catalogued | **no** — item JSON returns `resources: 0`, `url: None` |

So the free comparison is **1911 vs 1950**. The 1929 edition and the pasted-on 1950+
*updates* sit behind **ProQuest Digital Sanborn Maps**, which UC Berkeley subscribes to —
that is a concrete thing John's faculty credentials unlock and I cannot reach. Adding 1929
would split the 39-year gap and date the commercial strip's construction much more tightly.

**Sheet numbers are stable between the two editions here** — confirmed by reading each
edition's own street index rather than assuming:

- **sheet 179** — College Ave **even** side, 2800–2958 (Hillegass / Benvenue / College)
- **sheet 180** — College Ave **odd** side, 2801–2939 (College / Piedmont / Kelsey)

Both run Stuart → Russell → Ashby, i.e. the whole Elmwood commercial core. Sanborn's own
block numbers also carry across: 5365, 5364, 5363, **5359**, 4152, 4153, 4154 on sheet 180;
5333, 5347, 5332, **5346** on sheet 179.

## The finding: the Elmwood commercial strip did not exist in 1911

**1911, block 5359** (College east side, Russell to Ashby): the College Ave frontage is
**empty lots**. Four detached dwellings sit back in the block interior — 2909 and 2915
College, marked `D.` at 1–2 storeys, plus a 2½-storey house mid-block and one more at the
Ashby corner. The commercial strip is simply not there. Across Russell on block 5365, the
**College Ave M.E. Church** occupies the corner, and blocks 5364/5363 east of it are already
built out with dwellings and flats. So the residential Elmwood came first and the shops came
later — the strip was infill into an already-settled neighbourhood.

**1950, the same block:** continuously built, 2901 through 2953, plus the bank at the Ashby
corner. And the number in **every single footprint on that frontage is `1`.**

| | 1911 | 1950 |
|---|---|---|
| College frontage, Russell→Ashby | vacant lots | continuous storefronts |
| buildings on the frontage | 0 | ~10 structures, 2901–2953 + 2950 |
| storeys | — | **1 everywhere** |
| construction | — | frame → brick → reinforced concrete, north to south |
| water main in College | 2″ | 6″ |

Transcribed storey-by-storey in `data/reference/elmwood_sanborn_1950_storeys.csv`.

Detail worth keeping: the strip's construction material grades southward — **frame**
(yellow) at the Russell end, **brick** (pink) at 2941–2945, **reinforced concrete** (blue)
at 2947–2953 and the bank at 2950. That is a build-order signature; the concrete end went
up last. Uses in 1950 read store, store, store, restaurant, two offices, a rear **electrical
repairing** shop, an **A.F.A. hall**, a paint store, and the corner **bank** with a
mezzanine and a wood-truss roof. Behind the strip, the block interior is dwellings at 1, 2
and 2½ storeys plus a **6-apartment** building at 2711 Ashby.

**The claim this supports, carefully stated:** the Elmwood commercial strip was built
between 1911 and 1950, as single-storey buildings, and — comparing the 1950 footprints with
today's — it has not gained a storey since. The district's commercial core has been one
storey tall for something like three-quarters of a century. That is a *measured* statement
about built form, and it is the kind of thing the corridor debate argues about from
intuition.

**What it does not show.** It says nothing about whether adding storeys is desirable,
feasible, or economic. It does not date the strip more tightly than 1911–1950 (that needs
1929). And storey count is not floor area — the FAR series still needs footprint area,
which we have from Overture, multiplied by these counts.

## Next

1. **1929 via ProQuest** (John's UC login) — splits the gap, and would likely show the strip
   complete, dating it to the 1920s streetcar era.
2. **Georeference sheets 179/180** against the real city-block layer from
   `scripts/city_block_index.py` so historical footprints can be compared with modern
   Overture footprints on the same blocks.
3. **Historical FAR** = Sanborn storeys × footprint area ÷ block area, on blocks 5359 and
   5346, against the modern figures. That gives a *change-over-time* density series for the
   Elmwood instead of a single present-day snapshot.
4. Read the residential blocks (5333, 5347, 5332, 5364, 5363) the same way — they are the
   control group, and they show whether the residential fabric densified while the
   commercial strip stayed flat.
