**Status:** DESIGN (2026-10-12), REVISED same day: **John runs this on his own, separately from the
Berkeley 2050 working group** (his decision). Audiences: data journalists, the owners who pay the most,
and renters. Red-dot mailing list built (§6.4); nothing sent. Election day is **2026-11-03** (22 days);
ballots are already in the mail, so the campaign has two phases: **before the vote** (days 0–14) and

## 1. Who it's for, and what they need

Data journalists don't want our conclusion. They want to **check the numbers and find their own story** quickly:
raw tables they can load into their own tools, a method they can defend to an editor, the known weaknesses
stated up front, and someone who answers the phone. So the desk is **evidence first, argument second**. The
gap analysis (§4) is there because it's a fact pattern in the City's own documents, and each claim is
cited so a reporter can check it without trusting us.

**Disclosure (on every page and in every email):** this is John Gage's own work, not the working group's.
He co-authored the Berkeley 2050 op-eds and serves on the volunteer working group of independent advisors,
whose Evaluation No. 1 is linked; the group has not endorsed this desk or its mailings. Reporters will find
the affiliation anyway, so it's better they hear it first.

### 2.1 The 40-year figure (computed 2026-10-12; the model reproduces the City's $610M)

The bond is 40 years of levies (FY2027 to FY2066), three $100M tranches, $610M total debt service
(Res. 72,338-N.S. Exh. B). What one parcel pays over the 40 years depends on two things the City's
average rate hides: **how fast the citywide base grows** and **whether this parcel sells**. Show two
scenarios and never just one:

| parcel | **A. City's assumption** (base grows ~4.2%/yr; owner never sells, AV +2%/yr) | **B. Today's base** (no growth; upper bound) |
|---|---|---|
| median single-family (AV $700k) | **≈ $9,200** | ≈ $14,000 |
| recent buyer (AV $1.5M) | ≈ $19,600 | ≈ $30,000 |
| long-held (AV $250k) | ≈ $3,300 | ≈ $5,000 |

(Nominal dollars, not discounted. A parcel that sells mid-term resets to market value and pays more from
then on; the desk states that and doesn't model individual sales.)

**Conservation gate (the invariant, anchored per CLAUDE.md):** under scenario B, the sum of every
taxable parcel's 40-year payments must equal $610M × (taxable AV ÷ total AV). The build fails if it
doesn't. Scenario A is checked against the City's $22.14 average (it already is, in `checks()`).

### 3.2 Two data items to acquire before the levy-by-recipient column is honest

- **The 1% is not "paid to the County."** It is pooled and split among County, City, BUSD, Peralta,
  special districts and ERAF by the **AB 8 allocation factors for Berkeley's TRAs (13-0 … 13-5)**,
  published by the Alameda County Auditor-Controller. Without those factors the honest column is
  "1% general levy (shared)". Acquire the factors; then the County / City / BUSD shares of the 1% are
  computable per parcel.
- **Exemptions.** The homeowner's $7,000 exemption is in hand (the owner-occupied flag); nonprofit,
  welfare and veteran exemptions are not. Taxable AV is what the conservation gate needs.
- The per-sqft and per-parcel lines already map to recipients (Library tax → Library; BSEP + BUSD
  maintenance → BUSD; parks/fire/streets/paramedic → City). Rates were validated on single-family bills
  only; label non-residential estimates as such (the Sept-16 caveats).

## 4. "What the project list doesn't fund": analysis design

Three gaps, each built the same way, as a table: **need (City's own figure + document + page) · in
Measure U · gap · funded elsewhere?** Where the City has no figure, say so. That absence is a finding
(Evaluation No. 1, Q2). **No number goes in this section without a citation to a City or County document.**

### 4.1 Streets
- **Known from our record:** Vision 2050 rates the streets "at risk" (PCI 57); streets are absent from
  the project list, and our notes say they are "covered separately by Measure FF." **That claim must be
  verified before publication**: what FF funds, for how much, and for how long.
- **To source:** the City's latest pavement management report (MTC StreetSaver PCI, the deferred
  maintenance backlog, the annual spend needed to hold PCI vs actual); the CIP budget's paving line.
- **The chart:** backlog vs annual paving spend vs Measure U's $0 for paving; sidewalks ($14.6M, 50/50
  program) shown separately as what the list *does* fund in the right-of-way.

### 4.2 Fire stations and fire apparatus
- **Known:** Measure U funds Station 4 ($56.2M), Station 6 ($20.4M) and the training center ($30.1M) =
  $106.7M, against a **~$350M Fire facilities master-plan need** cited in the Evaluation.
- **To source:** the Fire Facilities Master Plan (which stations, condition, seismic status, cost each;
  which are left out); the **apparatus replacement schedule** (engines/trucks, ages, replacement cost,
  and the fund that pays for them; apparatus are usually equipment-replacement-fund or lease items, and
  GO bonds generally can't buy them, so say which and cite the City's own description).
- **The chart:** each station as a row: condition, master-plan cost, in Measure U? Apparatus as a
  second table: fleet age vs replacement cycle.

### 4.3 Operations and maintenance
- **Known:** no project carries an O&M estimate or a funding source (Evaluation Q2); $81.4M of new or
  expanded facilities implies $1.6–3.3M/yr new permanent O&M at the 2–4% NRC benchmark; $47.7M of the
  list wears out before the final levy in FY2066/67 (the ⟳ items); the General Fund faces $31M / $29M
  deficits (the measure's own findings).
- **To source:** current General Fund maintenance spending by asset class (adopted budget); the Asset
  Management Program (NexGen) status; the unfunded-need series ($882M → >$2.1B, 2020–2026, the
  discrepancy inventory) with each document cited.
- **The chart:** the 40-year levy timeline with the ⟳ assets' replacement dates overlaid. A turf field
  bought in 2027 is replaced around 2037, 2047 and 2057 while the 2027 tranche is still being repaid.

### 4.4 Rail
State the City's case for each project alongside the gap. The list does fund real needs (the seawall,
two stations, accessibility). The finding is the **gap between the City's documented need and this
list**, not that the list is worthless.

## 5. Story leads (starter queries ship with each)

1. Who pays the most? The 100 largest levies, and how many are new apartment buildings.
2. Same street, different bill: the Prop 13 spread on one block, in Measure U dollars.
3. Renters: apartments carry ~22% of the levy, much of it passed through.
4. The new-housing tax: buildings finished since 2018 (v4 completions) pay a share far above their
   share of parcels.
5. $22 or $67? The base-growth assumption behind the advertised rate, and who supplies the growth.
6. The turf field that's replaced three times while the bond is still being repaid.
7. Fire: three facilities funded, the rest of the master plan not; who pays for the engines?
8. Streets: PCI 57, $0 in this bond. What does Measure FF actually cover?
9. Who runs the new buildings? New O&M with no named revenue, in a deficit General Fund.
10. Oversight: two commissions and a triennial audit vs a Prop 39-style committee (Evaluation Q4).

## 6. Outreach: journalists by email, owners by post, renters through their organizations

### 6.0 Before any money is spent: FPPC
Acting alone doesn't exempt John from campaign law. Paying for printing or postage that opposes a ballot
measure is an **independent expenditure**. Once the total reaches **$1,000 in a calendar year**, John becomes
a committee. Inside 90 days of the election, IEs of $1,000+ are reported **within 24 hours**. More than
**200 substantially similar pieces in a calendar month** is a mass mailing, which needs sender
identification. Berkeley may add rules of its own (Berkeley Election Reform Act). **Get FPPC advice
first** (free; phone or written). Emails John writes and sends himself cost nothing and are the lowest-risk
channel. The 149 owner letters stay under the 200-piece threshold *in one month* if the renter mail is
not also sent that month, but the expenditure threshold still applies.

### 6.1 Journalists (unchanged): one-to-one email from John's Gmail
≈15–25 named reporters (list as in the first draft: Berkeleyside/Cityside, Daily Cal, Berkeley Scanner,
Chronicle, KQED, East Bay Times, Bay City News, CalMatters, Berkeley J-school data faculty). Plain text,
one link, one finding, disclosure line. Drafts go to Gmail drafts; John sends; each text is quoted to John
verbatim when drafted.

### 6.2 Owners who pay the most: a personal letter by post
**No email exists for these owners** in any dataset we hold, and buying addresses from a broker is ruled
out (spam filters; a "who sold you my address?" story). The County secured roll carries a **mailing
address for every parcel**, so post is the channel. One letter per mailing address, naming each of the
owner's parcels and its own figures:

> *Your parcel at [address], assessed at $[AV], would pay about **$[peak]/yr** for Measure U at the City's
> disclosed peak rate ($35 per $100k, from FY2040-41), and about **$[40yr A]** over the bond's 40 years
> on the City's own growth assumptions (up to **$[40yr B]** if Berkeley's tax base stops growing). Here's
> how we calculated it: [link to the parcel page]. Here's what the project list leaves out: [link].*

Then "paid for by" as the FPPC advises, and a return address. **For institutions (8) and the 42 parcels
with no owner name in our data**, address the letter "Owner of [situs]" at the roll's mailing address.

### 6.3 Renters: the message must match the rent law
**Berkeley appears to bar landlords from passing property-tax or bond increases through to
rent-controlled tenants** (2018 Chronicle report naming San Jose, Oakland, Berkeley). **Verify against BMC
13.76 / the Rent Board before any renter piece is written.** If confirmed:
- **Rent-controlled tenancies:** do NOT say "your rent goes up." Say: the owner pays; the cost can't be
  added to your rent; what it can do is squeeze the maintenance and operating budget of your building.
- **Exempt units** (built after 1980 (Costa-Hawkins), single-family houses, condos): rents are
  market-set, so a higher tax cost is one of the pressures on the next rent increase. That's an
  economic argument, not a line item. Say it that way.
- **Apartments (use class 7xxx) carry ~22% of the levy.**

Channels, cheapest and most credible first: the Berkeley Tenants Union and the Berkeley Property Owners
Association (landlords) as organizations, with a briefing and the data link; student media and ASUC
for the student-renter majority (~53% of Berkeley's poor are off-campus students, from the Snapshot);
carrier-route "Resident" mail (USPS EDDM) only if John decides to spend, after FPPC advice.

### 6.3a The renter join (BUILT 2026-10-12, private)
`scratch/2026-10-12/renter_join.py` (gitignored) joins the Rent Board unit registry (CPRA #26-2375, 2026-07-13,
41,279 units) to parcels and Measure U at the City's peak rate ($35/$100k). Outputs:
`renter_units_measure_u_peak35.csv` (per unit) and `renter_buildings_measure_u_peak35.csv` (per building).
- **Match:** 39,125 by APN (12-digit; 11-digit zero-padded; 10-digit = sub-parcel omitted → `00`), 870 by exact
  house number + street via `housing_rules.address.normalize_address` (flagged `matched_by=address`; ambiguous
  addresses refused), **1,284 unmatched** (216 APNs: stale or re-platted, per the standing guard). 96.9% matched.
- **Tenant-occupied units:** 27,221 at 5,514 parcels. The parcels they sit on carry ~$2.66M/yr of the levy at peak.
- **Even-split share per unit** (parcel levy ÷ registered units on the parcel), at peak:

| coverage | tenant units | p10 / median / p90 per unit per yr | subsidized | on mixed-use parcels | unit # missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fully Covered (rent ceilings) | 21,163 | $9 / **$52** / $141 | 1,004 | 891 | 13% |
| Partially Covered (market rent) | 6,058 | $0 / **$144** / $297 | 375 | 489 | 24% |

- The largest-levy buildings with tenants are all **Partially Covered** new construction: 2580 Bancroft (≈ $377/unit/yr),
  2125 University, 2108 Berkeley Way, 2150 Kittredge, 2035 Blake. The new-housing-pays-most finding, at the unit level.
- **Caveats:** the even split overstates the share on mixed-use parcels (commercial AV); $0 shares are units on parcels
  not yet assessed (new construction or ADUs: the assessment lag); unit numbers are missing on 13–24% of rows, so
  "Resident" mail to those buildings goes to the street address only. Subsidized tenants' rent is set by income,
  so they need a different message (or none).
