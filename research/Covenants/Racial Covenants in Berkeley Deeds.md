# Racial Covenants in Berkeley Deeds: Sources, Original Text, and Research

## How to find original deeds and extract covenant text

There is no single searchable database of Berkeley's original racially restrictive deeds yet, but there are three concrete paths to the primary documents:

**1. Alameda County Clerk-Recorder's Office (the deeds themselves)**
Official public records date back to 1853. Records from 1969 onward are indexed and searchable online; anything recorded before 1969 — which covers virtually all racial covenants (most run 1905–1948) — exists only on microfilm and must be searched in person at 1106 Madison Street, Oakland ([Alameda County Official Public Records](https://www.acgov.org/auditor/clerk/opr/opr.htm)). You look up the "instrument number" or book/page number, then request the physical film. The City of San Leandro's guide on this process notes that most discriminatory CC&Rs (covenants, conditions & restrictions) date from 1915–1948 ([City of San Leandro](https://www.sanleandro.org/1086/Redlining-and-Housing-Discrimination-His)).

**2. Alameda County's AB 1466 redaction program**
California's 2021 law (AB 1466) requires every county recorder to proactively find and redact racial covenants. Alameda County's implementation plan and "Restrictive Covenant Modification" (RCM) process are described on the [Auditor-Controller/Clerk-Recorder site](https://auditor.alamedacountyca.gov/clerk-recorder-real-property-covenant-modification/). Once a document is flagged, the county produces an RCM cover sheet with the original covenant text visible (before redaction) attached to the original instrument — these become the county's own historical register of covenants. Emailing CROCustomerService@acgov.org with a known book/page or instrument number is the fastest way to pull a specific document; there is no bulk public list yet for Alameda County (unlike Santa Clara, see below).

**3. The Bancroft Library — Mason-McDuffie Co. Records, 1904–1983**
This is the single richest primary source for verbatim Berkeley covenant text, because Mason-McDuffie developed most of the Berkeley Hills, Claremont, Northbrae, Thousand Oaks, and Berkeley View tracts. The finding aid (BANC MSS 89/12 c) is at the [Online Archive of California](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/tf800006jp/entire_text/); the collection includes deeds, restriction/title-policy maps, and legal records by tract (e.g., "Laurenita," "Shaw Tract"). Contact: bancref@library.berkeley.edu, (510) 642-6481. A 2014 student researcher (Maya Tulip Lorey, cited below) pulled specific container/folder numbers from this collection that you could request directly:
- Container 19, folder 3 — Place School Indenture, 1912
- Container 18, folder 33 — Agreement Regulating Use and Occupancy of Property, 1929
- Container 18, file 22 — Berkeley View promotional materials, 1935
- Container 18, file 13/14 — Declaration of Restrictions (1948) / Arlington Manor promotional material (1949)
- Volume 4, p. 11 — Claremont Park promotional pamphlet, 1905

## Verbatim covenant text already extracted from Berkeley deeds

These are direct quotations from primary Berkeley-area instruments, as reproduced in secondary sources with archival citations:

> "…if prior to the first day of January 1930 any person of African or Mongolian descent shall be allowed to purchase or lease said property or any part thereof, then this conveyance shall be and become void…"
— Claremont Park Company Indenture, 1910, quoted in [Berkeley City Council resolution](https://berkeleyca.gov/sites/default/files/2022-02/2021-02-23%20Item%2029%20Resolution%20to%20End%20Exclusionary.pdf) and in Lorey (2013/2014), citing the Alameda County Clerk-Recorder's pre-1969 microfilm index.

> "No part of the said lands now owned by the said Property Owners…shall ever be used or occupied by any person or persons of other than pure Caucasian blood. In the case persons of other than pure Caucasian blood are employed upon the premises as nurses or in domestic service by a person or persons of pure Caucasian blood, this agreement shall not apply."
— Agreement Regulating Use and Occupancy of Property, 1929, Mason-McDuffie Co. Records, BANC MSS 89/12 c, Bancroft Library, container 18, folder 33.

> "Said premises may be used for school purposes…on the further condition that…no children of African, Mongolian or Asiatic descent shall be admitted to said school."
— Place School Indenture, 1912, Mason-McDuffie Co. Records, container 19, folder 3.

> "…It is the purpose of Declarant by provisions hereof…to restrict the use and occupancy of said property to persons of a cultural status conducive to the creation and estimation of congenial friendship and fraternization between and among the occupants of said property…"
— Declaration of Restrictions, May 1948, Mason-McDuffie Co. Records, container 18, file 13 (this softer "cultural status" language reflects developers rewriting covenants after *Shelley v. Kraemer* made explicit racial language unenforceable).

> "This section is known as Arlington Heights, Thousand Oaks Heights and North Cragmont… Zoned first residential, single family, deed restrictions prohibit Asiatics and Negroes."
— HOLC 1937 Residential Security Map, Area A-1 description, quoted in the [Northbrae history site](https://northbraehistory.com/) and [Who Built America?](https://www.whobuiltamerica.org/item/redlining-maps-of-the-bay-area/), which also reproduces the Area D-2 (South/West Berkeley) narrative referencing "infiltration of Orientals" and "gradual infiltration of Negroes."

> "No negroes, no Chinese, no Japanese can build or lease in Rock Ridge Park."
— 1909 *San Francisco Call* advertisement for the Rockridge/Upper Rockridge tract abutting Berkeley, quoted in [East Bay Express, "Hidden Monuments to Racism"](https://eastbayexpress.com/hidden-monuments-to-racism-2-1/).

A more generic California-wide covenant template, cited by UC Berkeley Law as still found in property records statewide:
> "…That no part of any of said lots shall at any time be rented, leased, sub-leased or sublet to, or to be occupied or used by any person of either Hindu, African, Japanese, Chinese, or Mongolian descent, but such property shall be restricted to persons of the Caucasian Race forever…"
— [UC Berkeley Law, "So why does this matter now?"](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/archive/2021/09/Lack-Slides.pdf) (Jonathon Lack).

## Academic theses, papers, and class projects

**High school/independent research (heaviest archival digging on Berkeley specifically)**
- Maya Tulip Lorey, *A History of Residential Segregation in Berkeley, California, 1878–1960*, **The Concord Review**, 24(2), 2013/2014 ([full PDF](https://www.schoolinfosystem.org/pdf/2014/06/04SegregationinCA24-2.pdf)). This is the most citation-rich source found — a Concord Review paper (a journal for exceptional secondary-school history research) that personally pulled Mason-McDuffie deeds from the Bancroft Library and Alameda County Recorder's microfilm index and reproduces multiple full covenant clauses with box/folder citations (reproduced above).

**Peer-reviewed / graduate-level academic work**
- Marc A. Weiss, *Urban Land Developers and the Origins of Zoning Laws: The Case of Berkeley*, **Berkeley Planning Journal**, 3(1), 1986 ([eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt26b8d8zh/qt26b8d8zh_noSplash_38cfb754168650ea762c01485cf19053.pdf)). Draws on Weiss's book *The Rise of the Community Builders* (Columbia University Press, 1987); documents how the Berkeley Realty Board organized a 20-block covenant district explicitly as "protection against invasion of Negroes and Asiatics" (citing *California Real Estate* magazine, Feb. 1926 & Oct. 1926) and how Duncan McDuffie's zoning advocacy was intertwined with deed-restriction practice.
- Cole Manley, *The Subdivision and the Restrictive Covenant in Oakland, California, 1900–1930*, 2026, published via SAGE ([abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00961442261423523)). Manley is a History PhD student at UC Davis (BA Stanford, advised by Greg Downs); the article specifically covers how covenant-based subdivision development in the Oakland hills extended into Berkeley.
- Aradhya Sood & Ellen Solberg, *The Long Shadow of Housing Discrimination: Evidence from Racial Covenants*, working paper, 2023–2024 ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4606234); [PDF](https://aradhyasood.github.io/Sood_Solberg_Covenants_July2023.pdf)) — a quantitative economics paper on covenants' long-run effects, useful if you want a modeling framework for a Berkeley-specific analysis.
- Faiz Surani, Mirac Suzgun, Vyoma Raman, Christopher D. Manning, Peter Henderson, Daniel E. Ho, *AI for Scaling Legal Reform: Mapping and Redacting Racial Covenants in Santa Clara County*, arXiv 2025 / Journal of Law and the Innovation Economy 2026 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03888); [Stanford RegLab project site](https://reglab.github.io/racialcovenants/)). Not Berkeley/Alameda, but the most technically relevant paper for your civic-tech interests: Stanford's RegLab fine-tuned an open LLM to detect covenants across 24 million Santa Clara County deed documents, cutting manual review by an estimated 86,500 person-hours, and released their model publicly. Their methodology (OCR → keyword flagging → LLM classification → county counsel review → public historical registry) is directly reusable for an Alameda County/Berkeley pipeline.
- *A History of Restrictive Covenants in California*, California Supreme Court Historical Society Review, Spring 2025 ([PDF](https://www.cschs.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025-CSCHS-Review-Spring-Restrictive-Covenants.pdf)) — legal-history treatment of covenant enforceability in California courts, including *Shelley v. Kraemer*'s aftermath in the state.

**UC Berkeley class/institute projects (not a single "class project" but several institute-produced studies functioning like one)**
- Elizabeth Kneebone/Elsa Moore et al. (credited on the Northbrae site as "Moore, E. et al."), *Roots, Race and Place: A History of Racially Exclusionary Housing in the San Francisco Bay Area*, Haas Institute/Othering & Belonging Institute, UC Berkeley, October 2019 ([report page](https://belonging.berkeley.edu/rootsraceplace); [full PDF](https://belonging.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/ARCHIVE/haasinstitute_rootsraceplace_oct2019_publish.pdf)). This is the most comprehensive Bay Area-wide academic synthesis; it explicitly covers Berkeley's 1916 zoning ordinance (first single-family zoning in the U.S., celebrated by *California Real Estate* magazine for "protection against invasion of Negroes and Asiatics") and racial covenant/HOA practice through 1966.
- Anti-Eviction Mapping Project (AEMP), *Densifying Berkeley* report, 2022 ([PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/52b7d7a6e4b0b3e376ac8ea2/t/635830839d2601366841cfb4/1666723974882/AEMP_DensifyingBerkeleyReport_2022.pdf)) — a Berkeley-focused civic-research/mapping collective (adjacent to UC Berkeley scholars) tracing the throughline from Mason-McDuffie deed restrictions to 1916 zoning to present-day exclusionary zoning.
- The [Othering & Belonging Institute's Bay Area zoning dataset](https://github.com/OtheringBelonging/BayAreaZoning) on GitHub — open data companion to their zoning/segregation research, useful if you want to overlay covenant tract boundaries with contemporary zoning in a Datasette-style tool.

## Terner Center and other institute research

The Terner Center for Housing Innovation at UC Berkeley treats racial covenants as historical context rather than a primary research focus — it cites covenants and HOLC redlining as drivers of the persistent Black-white homeownership gap in reports such as *The Federal Government and the Black/White Homeownership Gap* (March 2021, [PDF](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Crisis-Response-Recovery-March-2021-Final.pdf)) and its *California 100* Facts-Origins-Trends report ([link](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/blog/future-of-housing-and-community-development-report-california-100/)). It has not published a Berkeley-specific covenant-mapping study — that role is filled instead by the Othering & Belonging Institute (also housed at UC Berkeley) and by outside legal/CS labs:

- **Othering & Belonging Institute (OBI), UC Berkeley** — *Roots, Race & Place* (above) is the closest thing to an institutional "Berkeley covenants" study; OBI also maintains an ongoing [Racial Segregation Research hub](https://belonging.berkeley.edu/racial-segregation-research) and a 2025-updated [national segregation report/map](https://belonging.berkeley.edu/landmark-berkeley-segregation-report-updated-latest-data).
- **Stanford RegLab** — the AI-based covenant-detection project described above; explicitly built to be reused by "the hundreds of jurisdictions" (including counties like Alameda) still working through AB 1466 compliance.
- **UC Berkeley School of Law** — hosts a recurring CLE-style presentation on covenant removal ("So why does this matter now?" by Jonathon Lack, Sept. 2021), which frames Berkeley's Claremont Court and Uplands developments alongside national examples.
- **Mapping Prejudice (University of Minnesota)**, **Mapping Racist Covenants (Tucson/Pima County)**, and the **UW Racial Restrictive Covenants Project (Seattle/Puget Sound)** are the leading out-of-state models for exactly the kind of crowdsourced, geocoded covenant database Berkeley doesn't yet have — their open-source pipelines are on GitHub ([UMNLibraries/mp-us-racial-covenants](https://github.com/UMNLibraries/mp-us-racial-covenants)) and could be adapted directly to Alameda County deed scans.

## Local/civic history sources with additional covenant detail

- Berkeley Historical Society & Museum, [Housing exhibit](https://exhibitsbhs.org/housing/) — covers covenants targeting both Black and Japanese American residents, plus California's 1913 Alien Land Law.
- [northbraehistory.com](https://northbraehistory.com/) — the single best short synthesis specifically for the Northbrae/North Berkeley Hills tracts, with citations to Lorey, Weiss, and OBI.
- City of Berkeley resolutions: [2020-12-01 Item 24 (striking covenants)](https://berkeleyca.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2020-12-01%20Item%2024%20Striking%20Racially%20Restrictive%20Covenants.pdf) and [2021-02-23 Item 29 (ending exclusionary zoning)](https://berkeleyca.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2021-02-23%20Item%2029%20Resolution%20to%20End%20Exclusionary%20-%20Supp2%20Harrison.pdf) — both quote the Mason-McDuffie 1930 clause and cite Weiss (1986).
- J. Barber, *Redlining: The history of Berkeley's segregated neighborhoods*, **Berkeleyside**, Sept. 20, 2018, and R. Gammon, *Hidden Monuments to Racism*, **East Bay Express**, Sept. 20, 2017 ([link](https://eastbayexpress.com/hidden-monuments-to-racism-2-1/)) — local journalism with additional advertisement text and interviews (e.g., historian Dennis Evanosky).
- Charles Wollenberg, *Berkeley: A City in History* (UC Press) — the standard academic city history, quoted by multiple sources above for the "almost all Asian and Black Berkeleyans lived south of Dwight Way and west of Grove Street" line.
- Grist, *Zoned out: One woman's half-century fight to desegregate Berkeley* (Dec. 2021, [link](https://grist.org/cities/zoned-out-one-womans-half-century-fight-to-desegregate-berkeley/)) — first-person account from Dorothy Walker and her husband Joe Kamiya, barred from living east of Grove Street in 1950.

## For your own civic-tech project

Given your work on berkeleybuild.com and Datasette-based civic tools, the most actionable next steps would be:
1. Request the specific Bancroft Library container/folder items listed above (they're a manageable, already-identified set) to get scanned original deed pages rather than secondary quotations.
2. Email Alameda County's Clerk-Recorder (CROCustomerService@acgov.org) for a status report on AB 1466 redactions completed to date — ask whether they maintain (or plan to maintain) a public historical register the way San Francisco and Santa Clara Counties are building.
3. Look at reusing Stanford RegLab's open covenant-detection model ([reglab.github.io/racialcovenants](https://reglab.github.io/racialcovenants/)) against Alameda County's own digitized pre-1969 deed images, if the county will share bulk scans — this is the same approach that took Santa Clara County from "infeasible to review manually" to a working public dataset.
